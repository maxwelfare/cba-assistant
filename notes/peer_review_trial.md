# Author-managed peer review: the middle tier

## 1. What it is, and who ruled

1.1 The owner, the owner's message of 2026-11-07 (2026-11-06 local): "One instance will just make up someting random and become attached to it. We have to have independent review and revision and asking hard questions. But can we do that with one session holding all the context, and adapting properly to the feedback? Kind of like how peer review actually works, with the original author managing the whole process?" After a trial he adopted it, 2026-11-07 (the owner's message of 2026-11-07): "We can call this a success. This is effectively the middle (or possibly middle-high) tier of effort and rigor."

1.2 The design is journal peer review, with an editor who is not the author: people who chose a course defend it (`staw_1976_knee`), and a separate person ruling on whether to go on is the remedy (`simonson_1992_deescalation`). The US standard for reviewing influential analyses makes public "the peer reviewers' report(s), and the agency's response to the peer reviewers' report(s)" (`omb_2004_peer_review_bulletin`).

1.3 A case reaches this tier from its fast draft by default: the fast reviews and the editor's decision letter on them stand as milestone 1's first round, and the author's revision answers the letter. Each milestone then has its own round: milestone 1's second round reviews the technical appendix with every result an empty placeholder, and its editor rules, before the paper and the summary are written; milestone 2's round reviews the results and the two documents. The seats in order, and the brief each is sent, are `notes/fast_estimate.md` 1.3.

## 2. The roles

2.1 The author: one agent per case, which holds the whole case, writes and revises everything, and answers every reviewer's point in a response letter: accepted, revised (changed otherwise than asked), declined, or for the user, saying where it changed or why it did not. The letter opens with a table of every point and its status, the comment resolution matrix, printed from the case's record of review points, `review/points.toml` (`cases/README.md`), a row per point that the editor rules and the author answers, carried forward from round to round; that record is the record of closed points of the shared `standards/process/peer_review.md` 3.7, handed to every later reviewing seat, and a point an editor has closed stays closed unless a reviewer names a ground of that item (`notes/briefs/author_upgrade.md`, message A, 3.1 and 3.9). It runs its own checks. It does not review its own work beyond them. The author delegates the reading: it sends helper agents, in parallel, to fetch, store and quote sources and to answer narrow questions about files, and keeps its own context for the design, the model and the writing (the owner, 2026-11-07: "probably we should nudge them to delegating"; an author that does not delegate can run out of context mid-task). Before it writes code it reads `cost_benefit_engine/README.md` (the toolbox's catalogue) and the list of toolbox candidates, notes/toolbox_candidates.md, where it exists, and uses, or copies into the case and edits, what fits, to save its own context (the owner, 2026-11-07: "make a toolbox that authors are informed of, and encouraged to use to save their context"); it never edits `cost_benefit_engine/` during a case. After the editor's ruling and the last revision, it adds to the list of toolbox candidates, notes/toolbox_candidates.md (made if it does not exist), one line for each general-purpose piece it built, and marks any candidate it copied with its case's name (the owner, same words, the owner's message of 2026-11-07: "have a system to promote to the shared toolbox anything new that authors make along the way"); a candidate a second case copies is promoted through the shared `build` workflow, by the roadmap's toolbox item.

2.2 The referees: two for milestone 1 and one for milestone 2 (3.2), fresh agents, each blind to the others and to the author's reasoning until a later round hands it the record of closed points. Each is told to assume the analysis has turned out wrong and to say why (a pre-mortem, `klein_2007_premortem`), and follows the shared `.claude/agents/referee.md`. One reviews the economics: structure, method, inputs and what would make the result wrong. The other audits: every number traced to its source, the code computing what the appendix says, and the model rerun. The second round of milestone 1 goes to the same two, resumed with their memory, and asks only whether their own points were answered. A new point counts there only if it could move a headline result outside its 90 per cent interval, if it could change which option ranks first, or if it is a stated falsehood.

2.3 The editor: a fresh agent for each ruling. It reads the reports, the response letter and the changes. It rules on each point (settled, a note in the Limits with its size, or still to be done), writing its ruling, the point's state and a limit's text into the point's row of the record and on the whole (accept, or another round). Its test is the rigor the technical appendix states at its top and rule 5 of `notes/owner_cba_rules.md` (the seat's summary of his post, not his words): stop refining when the next refinement is worth less than it costs. A finding on the user's rulings, on a value judgement or on what the case is for is the user's: the editor lists it for a decision card and never decides it.

2.4 The office: the coordinator session. It dispatches the seats and passes file paths between them. It changes nothing in the work.

2.5 Every effect, in every case, is listed by three blind `effects-lister` agents and estimated by hand in the fast draft, and the decision letter names the few effects that get a model, each with about six inputs (`notes/fast_estimate.md` 2.1 and 2.4). The advocate, the opponent and the editor of effects that this item held were deleted on 2026-11-10: the listers and the estimators take their place, and the work of the editor of effects moves to the fast editor and the referees.

## 3. The milestones

3.1 M1, the analysis: design, inputs, model, results with intervals, and the technical appendix (an academic paper with every citation and formula, ending with its record of choices, requirements and review, `notes/briefs/author_upgrade.md`, message A, 2.1). Its method is reviewed before it is built: each decision of method that can move the result goes to its own referee, and the purpose check reads the results and an outline of the paper before any document is written (`notes/fast_estimate.md` 1.3.4). The appendix its second round reads is written as a template, every results table and result number a placeholder of `results/facts.json` and the results section shells with no paragraph describing them, so that the method is judged before any result is seen; the editor's check names the accepted commit, from which the run of record is made (`notes/briefs/author_upgrade.md`, message A, 2).

3.2 M2, the policy paper (about 20 pages for educated non-specialists) and the one-page executive summary, written once milestone 1 is accepted, with the appendix's results paragraphs, from the accepted appendix and the run of record. One referee reviews them and the appendix's results section, for faithfulness to the appendix and for plain language, and checks that the method that ran is the one accepted: it reruns the case from the commit milestone 1's check names and diffs the model, the inputs and the estimates against it; then the editor rules (`notes/briefs/author_documents.md`, messages B and C). Milestone 1's matrix carries forward and is handed to the referee, whose review is confined to the results and the documents read against the accepted milestone 1 (the shared `standards/process/peer_review.md` 3.8): what it closed stays closed, and a milestone 2 point declined or ruled against its referee joins the appendix's record of review.

## 4. Files

4.1 A case is written in its own folder, `cases/<case>/`, on `main`: its documents in `documents/`, and its review record in `review/`, each file named in the brief of the seat that writes it.
