# The fast tier, and the order of a case's seats

## 1. What it is, and who ruled

1.1 The owner, 2026-11-07: "A fast estimate would not have had any mid process revision, just reviewer notes at the end (we should have tested that first)." His earlier description, 2026-11-06: "just instances with the right onboarding going straight through the process. Then they have a deliverable to the user, and after it's done all the reviewers at each round give their comments, so the user can see the first draft and all the review comments and talk about the next steps forward."

1.2 The established form is the rapid review, which cuts steps of a full review and says which it cut (`garritty_2024_updated`). This tier cuts revision during the run, and keeps every reviewer. It is broad but shallow, by the owner's ruling of 2026-11-10 (the owner's message of 2026-11-10: "I want a 'broad but shallow' approach"): "every analysis, even the fast one, should start with subagents listing all the good and bad effects of the policy. Then the author does a BOTEC fermi-style estimate for all of those ... No Python code is allowed to be written at this stage"; only the effects the fast review names then get a model, "very simple, maybe half a dozen inputs each, and math that could fit on one spreadsheet tab". Why, and the paragraph every seat carries: `notes/briefs/simplicity.md`. The literature: `research/broad_shallow_botec.md` and `research/why_broad_shallow.md`.

1.3 A case runs at this tier first, and the same author then upgrades it to the middle tier of `notes/peer_review_trial.md` (the order, fast first, is the seat's; the owner, 2026-11-07, of the middle tier: "I am satisfied enough with this to make it the default, 13% more is fine to give the user something much sooner for them to review and comment on"). In an exploratory analysis the requirements conversation first runs the `facts-researcher` agents (`facts_record.md`), designs the options with the user and drafts them (the `scope-case` skill's section 1), so the author starts from drafted options. The seats in order, each with its brief in `notes/briefs/`. A seat named with an agent type is dispatched as that type, defined in `.claude/agents/`, with its brief as the task; the definition holds what is the same at every dispatch and sets the model and effort. A seat named without one is a general agent, its brief ending with the welfare paragraph:
1.3.1 the author, a `case-author`, writes the fast draft (2.1) from the lists and estimates the requirements conversation made, where the session dispatches the `effects-lister` and `fermi-estimator` agents and pools the lists itself (the `scope-case` skill's 0.5.3 to 0.5.7), checking the pool and the estimates and dispatching listers or estimators only for what is missing: `author_fast.md`;
1.3.2 three reviewers comment on it (2.2): the economics referee, the shared `referee` agent type (`.claude/agents/referee.md`), `review_fast_econ.md`; an `audit-referee`, `review_fast_audit.md`; and a `documents-referee`, `review_fast_documents.md`;
1.3.3 a fresh `editor` turns their comments, with any the user has made, into one decision letter, which names the effects that get a model (2.4): `editor_letter.md`;
1.3.4 the same author, resumed, models those effects and revises to milestone 1 of the middle tier (2.5), answering the letter: `author_upgrade.md`, its message A. Its review comes before the work it reviews, in three steps, since a referee put to one decision before it is built has changed the most for the least, at $2 to $3 a decision, where a fault found in a written document costs a rewrite (roadmap R2):
1.3.4.1 before building any model, the author lists its decisions of method (message A, 3.0); each it marks as able to move the result goes to a shared `referee`, one referee to one decision, all in parallel, and a decision far inside the result's uncertainty goes to none (`notes/what_a_case_delivers.md`). Each referee's task names three things in this order (CORE 4.6): first the settled record, which it reads first and does not reopen: the case's `requirements.md`, the editor's letter on the fast draft (`review/m1_r1_editor_letter.md`) and, in a later version, the last ruling's record of closed points; then the decision, in the one line the list states it in, and the artifacts it rests on: `design.md`, `estimates.toml` and the stored sources they cite; and last the author's entry for that decision in `review/m1_method_decisions.md`, its options, choice and reasons, which the referee reads only after its own reading of the decision is written. It checks the claims the decision rests on in the table and the stored text itself, and writes `review/m1_method_<n>.md` and no other file;
1.3.4.2 the author, resumed with their reports, answers them, builds the models, reruns the total tool and drafts a one-page outline of what the paper will say (message A, 3.13);
1.3.4.3 before any document is written, the purpose check, a shared `referee` with `purpose_check.md` as its task, reads the results and the outline against the owner's rules and the case's requirements (the owner, 2026-11-12: "add to the handoff after the model is built (and possibly after a paper outline is drafted?) but before the work goes into writing it"); the author, resumed with its report, makes the fixes it marks as the author's and writes the appendix. On the verdict `stop for the owner`, the coordinator first puts each finding the report marks as the owner's (or the case's user's) as a decision card, and the author writes once they are answered. The purpose check runs once for each build or re-estimation of the model, before the documents are written from it: here, and in each later revision of an accepted case (`notes/workflows/revise_case.js`, its step 2). A further round of review under 1.3.5 to 1.3.7, and the fixes and checks after a ruling, read the documents against a model it has already checked, and do not run it again;
1.3.5 the economics and audit referees of the fast draft, resumed, review milestone 1 (`notes/peer_review_trial.md` 3.1): the technical appendix as its template, every result in it a placeholder of the facts file and its results section shells, read with the design, the estimates, the models and their sources, so that the method is judged before any result is written about: `review_r2.md`;
1.3.6 a fresh `editor` rules on that round: `editor_ruling.md`, its message A. If it accepts with fixes, the author makes them (message B of the brief of 1.3.4) and the same editor checks them (`editor_ruling.md`, message B), whose final line names the accepted commit, from which the run of record is made; if it rules another round, 1.3.5 and 1.3.6 repeat with the round raised;
1.3.7 milestone 2, the policy paper and the one-page summary, by `notes/peer_review_trial.md` 3.2, once the editor's check accepts milestone 1. Where the case's requirements name a reader no profile in `audiences/` describes, the session first dispatches one `facts-researcher` to write that profile (`notes/audience_layer.md` 1.3), so that it exists when `author_documents.md`, message A, 2 reads it. The same author, resumed, fills all three documents from the generated facts file, writes the appendix's results paragraphs and the paper and the summary, with a `worked-example` agent for each document's lead, and builds and looks after every edit. A fresh `documents-referee` then reads the paper, the summary and the appendix's results section, for faithfulness and plain language, and checks that the method that ran is the one accepted, rerunning the case from the commit milestone 1's check names; a fresh `editor` rules and checks any fixes: `author_documents.md`, its messages A to E. After the editor's message C ruling, the fixes and checks (messages D and E, two rounds at most) run as the workflow `notes/workflows/revise_case.js` with `firstUpgrade: true`, the ruling's return as `ruling`, and the args `root`, `caseDir`, `caseName`, `caseLine` and `rulings`. Another round, if the editor rules one, repeats the review and the ruling with the round raised;
1.3.8 where the user asks for them, peer review packages for human peer reviewers, always an option and never on by default, nudged more the more rigorous the level (`presets.md` 10.3, row 6; the owner, 2026-11-10: "Reviewer packets are always an option, but the more rigorous the analysis is the more the assistant should nudge the user into making them and sending them out"): the author of the last revision, resumed after the level's last ruling (after a sketch, a `case-author` dispatched for them; after Fast, the editor's letter of 1.3.3; at Normal the check of 1.3.7's editor; at Full that on the systematic review's update, `systematic_input_review.md` 10.3, the peer review packages being made again where that review is added after them), makes one for each reviewer's field (the owner, 2026-11-02: "packets for peer reviewers, explaining the overall process and asking for them to audit their expertise"): `reviewer_packet.md`;
1.3.9 at every level from Fast up, a `pdf-layout` agent, by its checklist `notes/pdf_layout_review.md`, once a case's documents are final and built, after the author's last fixes to them and before any document goes to the user, and again after any later edit to a document. It reports and edits nothing (`notes/pdf_layout_review.md` 6): a finding in a layout file or in `tools/typeset.py` goes to a `build` of the generator, after which the documents are built again; its findings of content go, at Fast, into the reviewers' notes the user is shown, and from Normal up to the author with the editor's fixes.
1.3.10 where the case's requirements name a reader the documents are voiced for, an audience edition of the summary and the policy paper, never of the appendix (`notes/audience_layer.md` section 4): an `audience-translator` with the reader's profile (`audiences/<key>.toml`) once the plain documents are accepted and laid out, then one round of review and the editor's ruling by `notes/briefs/audience_review.md`, then `pdf-layout` on the edition. The plain edition is the record and travels with it.
The run goes straight through, as far as the level the user chose, which the case's `requirements.md` names (`.claude/skills/scope-case/presets.md` 10.1 and 10.4, the owner's four levels of 2026-11-10): a sketch is made inside the requirements conversation and runs no seat of 1.3; Fast stops after 1.3.3 and the layout review of 1.3.9, with the draft, the three reviews and the letter as its deliverable, the letter's points serving as the user's shortlist of refinements, and resumes at 1.3.4 with the same author if the user asks; Normal, the default, runs all of 1.3, pausing after the letter on the fast draft only at a user's item the letter marks as changing which effects get a model (`notes/briefs/editor_letter.md` item 14) until the user answers it, since a model the answer would drop costs more than the wait, and carrying on past every other user's item; where the user is shown the fast draft as soon as it is ready, it is shown as its Markdown, marked unreviewed, with no build and no layout review; Full adds, after Normal, the systematic review of the inputs the user picks (`systematic_input_review.md`). The run never waits for a data key: the coordinator puts each entry of the case's `data_wanted.md` (the `case-author` definition's Method 3.0.3) to the user as one decision card, saying what the source adds, what going without it costs and the steps from its row of `shared/library/access.toml`, at the user's next attention, and a key set before the author's next dispatch is used then. A spreadsheet, where `requirements.md` asks for one, is built after the step it names, 1.3.3 or the end of the run, by the `sheet-twin` agent (`presets.md` 10.3, row 1): one tab of all the estimates, and one tab for each model. When a normal draft has finished, the coordinator offers the expert survey as one decision card (`presets.md` 10.6); its questions are the factors and inputs whose source is judgement.

## 2. The seats

2.1 The author: one agent, which delegates its reading (`notes/peer_review_trial.md` 2.1) and at this tier writes no code. Straight through:
2.1.1 The effects. Where a requirements conversation ran, it has dispatched three `effects-lister` agents at once and pooled their lists (the `scope-case` skill's 0.5.3 to 0.5.7), and the author checks the pool; where none ran, or it was cut short, the author dispatches them and pools. Each lister is shown only the policy and its base case, copied from `design.md` word for word, and one angle: every group affected, one by one; the effects of the effects over time (the futures wheel); and what opponents, or those who bear the costs, would raise (the premortem). They work blind to each other, in words only. Where the case's requirements say that searches name the case by kind only, every lister, estimator and fetcher is told so at dispatch. Their lists are pooled, with the effects the design names, into one impact inventory, merging duplicates and an effect with its own consequence so that nothing is counted twice, and marking each transfer, which moves to the account of who gains and who loses while the real costs that come with it stay effects of their own (the nominal group technique and the impact inventory, `research/broad_shallow_botec.md` section 2).
2.1.2 The estimates. Where a requirements conversation ran, it has dispatched `fermi-estimator` agents over the pooled list, several in parallel where it is long, and the author checks the estimates and has only the missing ones made; where none ran, or it was cut short, the author dispatches them. Before any estimator is sent, whoever dispatches them fixes in `design.md` the name and the values of every quantity more than one effect shares, gives each estimator that sheet, and tells each the level the case runs at, so that from the fast estimate up it names each factor's form (`notes/factor_forms.md`). The author marks the effects that look largest. Each estimate multiplies a few factors, about five at most, each with a low and a high that the truth has a 1 in 20 chance of passing on each side, a middle, a unit, and a source key or the word "judgement"; its low and high are the least and greatest products of its factors' lows and highs, which with every factor positive are the product of the lows and of the highs, and its middle the product of the middles; the arithmetic is written out so that a person can redo it; the largest effects get a second chain from a different start, entered as a second row with a weight; and an effect no source reaches is still estimated, as a judged chance and a size whose high it passes one time in twenty, its tail running on beyond the high, where much of a wide factor's expected value lies, and enters the total (the `fermi-estimator` definition, rule 9). The method is the `fermi-estimator` definition. Each source an estimator names with its address, whoever dispatched the estimator has a `source-fetcher` store, dispatched and its fragment merged by the `case-author` definition's item 6.
2.1.3 The draft. It writes every estimate into the case's `estimates.toml` (3.1), runs the total tool (3.2), and writes a short technical note and a one-page summary from them: each effect explained as a one-paragraph example calculation; the total's expected value with its 90 per cent interval; how many of the effects of largest positive expected value could be removed before the expected net benefits turn negative. They name each range in plain words: an effect's printed low and high are the least and greatest it could be with each factor at its low or its high ("if every factor were at its low at once", or at its high, where all are positive); an effect that can go either way is described by its chance of being above zero and its mean on each side, never by a ratio to its middle; the total's interval is its "90% interval", with the number of draws stated once near the first result and a draw explained where the word is first used (the `case-author` definition's Method 8.3); expected values add across effects and middles do not; and for each effect the tool flags, whose expected value is a large multiple of its middle, they say how much of that value comes from draws above its 95th percentile. It does not stop for review, and it works in proportion: well under a middle-tier case, which has cost about $49 to $72.

2.2 The reviewers, after the author is done, each blind to the others: an economics referee (a pre-mortem, `klein_2007_premortem`, by the shared `.claude/agents/referee.md`), an audit referee (every factor traced to its source, every product checked, the total tool rerun), and a documents referee (faithfulness and plain language). Each report opens with the size check of 2.6 and then how the analysis could be simpler, before what is missing, what is counted twice, and whether each estimate's arithmetic and sources hold. Each numbers its comments. Each comment names the step it would send work back to (effects, estimates, model or documents), and says how far it could move the expected total and the ranking of the options, whether that takes the total outside its 90 per cent interval, and whether it is a stated falsehood. No comment is acted on during the run.

2.3 The office: the coordinator session dispatches the seats and changes nothing in the work. What falls to it, since no seat does it:
2.3.1 A source a seat without storing tools names in its return, with its address, the office has stored by a `source-fetcher`, dispatched and its fragment merged by the `case-author` definition's item 6.
2.3.2 It commits what no seat commits: at Fast, the three reviews, the editor's letter and the record of review points, `review/points.toml`; at every level, the generator fixes that the layout review's findings led to, the built PDFs and the case's `versions.toml`.
2.3.3 It tags the version and pushes the tag (`notes/case_pipeline.md` 2.4.2 and 2.4.3).

2.4 Which effects get a model: no fixed count. The editor's letter takes the effects in order of their share of the total's variance, which the total tool prints, and weighs first each effect `requirements.md` records the user flagged for attention, naming it or saying why not. That share is an effect's covariance with the total over the total's variance, an allocation of the variance that sums to one. For an effect that shares no drawn quantity with another, this share is its main-effect variance, the value of perfect information under squared-error loss (Oakley and O'Hagan 2004, 2.5.2); where effects share one, the share is an allocation, and the shared quantity is ranked by its own main-effect index, which the tool prints beside every total, of a table with or without policies, for every quantity two or more effects rest on: a factor of their chains, the chance a complement is one minus, a start or a number of years with the drawn rate that discounts it, and, where the weighted total leads, a weight's incomes and elasticity. Each share and each index carries its Monte Carlo standard error from 10 batches of the draws; under a heavy tail these are wide, and shares within about two standard errors of each other are ties. Going down the ranking, the letter names an effect for a model while a model would add what its estimate lacks and what it adds is worth the model's cost in dollars, from what earlier models are recorded to have cost, where a record gives it. A model adds one of two things. It may bring evidence, such as a dataset, a time path or a threshold, and so narrow the total's spread (the seat's gloss; the owner's rule 5 of `notes/owner_cba_rules.md` says only to stop refining when the next refinement is worth less than it costs). Or it may remove a bias or a wrong structure, such as one quantity that several rows state apart with different values, or rows that add across different futures; its worth is then how far the reviews say the correction moves the expected total or the ranking of the options, which the share of variance does not measure. Where the case's requirements name a bar, such as the best other use of the money (the owner's rule 16), value of information is "defined with respect to the relevant utility or loss function and the available decisions" (`oakley_2004_probabilistic`, 2.5.2), and the engine's `evppi` (`cost_benefit_engine/value_of_information.py`) gives it from the draws. The letter also names, for a second chain by hand, each effect with one chain that carries a large share of the expected total, since a confident range can be wrong without being wide (`soll_2004_overconfidence`).

2.5 The middle tier. Only the effects the letter named get a model in Python, each about six inputs with arithmetic that fits on one spreadsheet tab; every other effect keeps its estimate, and the total tool adds the models' draws to the estimates. The middle tier's peer review runs as `notes/peer_review_trial.md` sets it, one round for each milestone.

2.6 Size is checked, not only urged. Every referee checks first that each estimate has about five factors at most and each model about six inputs on one tab, and that a new version has no more inputs across its models than the one before unless the user's requirements ask for more.

## 3. The table of estimates, and the total tool

3.1 The table of estimates, the contract between the `fermi-estimator`, the author and the total tool, at `cases/<case>/estimates.toml`: one `[[effect]]` per effect, with `name`, `kind` ("benefit" or "cost"), `who`, and one `[[effect.factor]]` per factor with `name`, `unit`, `low`, `middle`, `high`, `form` (lognormal, the default, a two-piece lognormal joined at the median, normal, logit-normal or bernoulli, a fact drawn whole, never a default; `notes/factor_forms.md`) and `source`, a low at or below zero only under form normal; or with `name` and `complement_of` alone, one minus a chance named there. `kind` is the direction a positive value counts, and chains of one effect may differ in it. A factor of unit "probability", or of a share (unit "share" or beginning "share "), with no form is a logit-normal, and a chance names no form but logit-normal or bernoulli. The tool refuses any key it does not know, naming it. An effect may state its own `low`, `middle` and `high`, the estimator's hand products, which the tool checks and replaces (3.2). Where the case has policies, they share the one table: each effect carries `policy`, the name of the policy it belongs to, or a list of two or more names for an effect that happens only where all of them are in force (how an interaction is stated, as a row of its own), and an effect with none, such as a cost every set that does something bears, counts in every set that holds a policy and never in the base, the set of no policy, which is the world without action, so the base's net benefits are zero in every draw (OMB Circular A-4's baseline); a top-level `[lever]` lists each lever's mutually exclusive levels (`scale = ["small", "large"]`). `option`, the older name, is a policy of one lever, so rival whole designs are one lever. A second chain is a second `[[effect]]` of the same name, each row with a `weight`, the weights of one name summing to 1. A factor name used in more than one effect is one quantity, with the same values wherever it appears; it may be stated once as a top-level `[factors.<name>]` table (`unit`, `low`, `middle`, `high`, optionally `form`, `source`, `currency` and `price_year`), each row then naming it by `name` alone, and a row that restates a value must match it. A modelled effect adds `draws`, the path of its model's draws, written by `cost_benefit_engine.sampling.write_draws` at the model's seed, count and method, and keeps its factors as the estimate the model replaced. A model input that is a quantity of the table keeps the factor's name, so the total tool draws it on the same random numbers as the model, and that factor states the model input's own specification, `distribution` and its fields, so the two are drawn alike draw by draw; where `inputs.toml` stands beside the table, the tool refuses a factor whose distribution differs from the model input of the same name, or that states low, middle and high in its place, a yes-or-no fact's chance (below) excepted. A factor may name any family the engine has (`cases/README.md`, "An input") in place of `low`, `middle`, `high` and `form`: `distribution` and that family's fields, with `unit` and `source`, where the quantity's shape has a source, such as a fat-tailed event size following a power law (`distribution = "power law"`, `min`, `alpha`); low, middle and high stay the default for a quick judgement. The tool draws it with the engine's quantile function on the factor's own random numbers, uses its computed 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles wherever it uses a low, middle and high, refuses a family whose mean is infinite, and refuses a factor stating both forms or neither (`notes/factor_forms.md`). The total tool draws a yes-or-no fact (form bernoulli) in two stages, its chance and then the fact against a second uniform number of its own, and computes no mean by formula, so a fact that the modelled effect depends on is two model inputs: its chance, under the fact's name, with the factor's low, middle and high as its 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles, and `<name> (so or not)`, of kind uniform from 0 to 1; the model takes the fact as yes in a draw where the second is below the first, so it draws the fact on the table's random numbers, the same draw as any row that names the fact, to the closeness of the two chances' distributions; the total tool refuses a bernoulli factor, or its complement, on a `draws` row. No effect stays outside the total: one no source reaches is estimated as a judged chance and size like any other (the `fermi-estimator` definition, rule 9). The form, with an example, is the `fermi-estimator` definition's.

3.1.1 Who gains and pays, time and money (rule 12 of his post "CBA 301" and rules 14 to 17 of his post "Welfare Estimation 201", as `notes/owner_cba_rules.md` states them, the latter as the literature states them; scope rule 1). The author writes these top-level keys in `estimates/changes.toml`, and the assembler puts them at the head of the table (3.6); the tool's opening lines give the method:
- `year`: the one year money is priced in and discounted to.
- `currency`: the table's currency, three letters; money in another is refused until a case needs it.
- `guidance`: the pinned edition, `"<jurisdiction>/<edition>"` of `jurisdiction_guidance/`, whose price index converts money and each of whose discount rates or schedules is a reading of its own.
- `discount_rate`: the name of the factor of the drawn discount rate, a distribution with its source, whose reading leads.
- `[groups.<name>]`: a few groups of people, each with `income`, the name of the factor of its income, and `public = true` for one whose money is public.
- `reference`: the group whose money weighs 1.
- `elasticity`: the name of the factor of the income elasticity of the value of a statistical life, judged for the case's range of incomes from `hammitt_2019_valuing`; required wherever rows fall in two or more groups.
- `common_values`: the factors already at a common rate (the guidance's value of a statistical life or life-year), whose rows are not weighted again.
- `lead = "dollars"`: set where the requirements bind the case to guidance that weighs no one, so the dollar total leads; otherwise the weighted total leads.
- `life_year`: the name of the factor of a life-year's value in the reference group's money, which gives each reading's weighted total in life-years.
- `bar_group`: the group the same public money would reach as cash, the bar of the owner's rule 16, which the tool prints beside the total.
A row adds `group`, one of the groups, beside its free-text `who`; `start` and `years`, each naming a factor (3.1's timing: the `fermi-estimator` definition, 6.4); and a money factor carries `currency` and `price_year`, its unit naming no currency. A transfer is two rows of the same named factors; the excess burden of public money is the tool's reading, never a row. A `draws` row in a table with `discount_rate` names one file per reading, by the reading's label.

3.2 The total tool, `tools/estimate_total.py`, run from the project root on a table's path; it prints to two significant figures, with the values of one range and the options' totals at the figures that tell them apart, and writes the same numbers, every digit kept, to `cases/<case>/results/estimate_total.json` (or with `--out DIR` to `DIR/estimate_total.json`, nothing written beside the table), and with `--draws` also each option's leading total in every draw to `cases/<case>/results/estimate_total_draws/<option key>.txt` (`total.txt` with no policy; for a table of more than one lever or free policy, one file per evaluated set, its policies joined by `+`, `base.txt` the base), from which a case computes what resolving a question is worth. It computes each effect's low and high as the least and greatest products over its factors' lows and highs, and its middle as the product of their middles. A stated value is a check on the hand arithmetic and nothing else. The estimators state their numbers to two figures, and one rounding to two figures moves a number by at most 5 per cent, so a stated value within 10 per cent of the product passes, the product takes its place, and the run prints how many blocks it restated. One further off is refused, with the effect, the stated value and the product, since that is a slip a person should see; a product rounded to a single figure can be that far off, so a stated product keeps two. Every mean it reports is the mean of the draws, printed and written with its Monte Carlo standard error; it computes none by formula. It reports: each effect's printed range, its expected value, its median and its share of the total's variance, the shares summing to one; the total's expected value, its median, its 90 per cent interval and the chance it is positive; how many of the largest benefits can be removed before the expected net benefits turn negative; a flag on any effect whose expected value is a large multiple of its middle, with the share of that value from draws above the effect's 95th percentile; and each effect's 90 per cent interval, its share of draws above zero and its mean on each side; for an effect whose draws take both signs, its tail share and no flag. It reports the totals at every reading, the leading one (the drawn rate) first, each in dollars and, where rows fall in two or more groups, weighted; the account by group; the public outlay, the net cost to the groups marked public, with net benefits per public dollar; and, where the edition gives one, the excess-burden reading of the dollar total. Its method is stated in its own opening lines, and in words in the JSON's `method`, which the appendix prints (`{{TABLE method_annex}}`, `cost_benefit_engine/documents.py`); its `factors` gives each factor's form and the mean of its draws. A seat copies a number it shows from the printed output and works any further number from the stored file, never from a printed figure or from memory (the `case-author` definition's Method 8).

3.3 Policies and levers (roadmap R15). The tool evaluates sets of policies, each a set's own effects with the joint effects of policies it holds and, in every set but the base, those of no policy, a set holding at most one level of each lever. It draws every factor once per draw for the whole table, so every set is evaluated on common draws: a quantity two policies share has one value in both, and an effect of one name with two chains takes the same chain in every set in a draw. Where the allowed sets number at most 1,024 (ten free policies) it evaluates every one; above that, the base, every policy alone and every allowed pair, then a search led by the policies of high value alone (the tool's opening lines say how, and what it can miss). It reports each set's outlay and total; each policy added to the base; the leading set, of highest expected net benefits, and each of its policies removed from it; each pair's interaction, NB(p + q) − NB(p) − NB(q) + NB(base), the mean of the per-draw interaction, exactly zero where no row enters it; each with the standard error of its own per-draw difference on the common draws; and the frontier of public outlay (the net cost to the groups marked public) against expected net benefits, the sets no other beats on both, ranked on the leading total. A table of one lever, such as rival whole designs, also reports each level apart, as `options`, with no total across levels, and on the same draws how often each level's total is the highest and, for each ordered pair, how often the first's total is above the second's. No table of one option less another is written. A policy that changes a factor rather than owning rows is not yet built (2.64.4).

3.4 The quiet print. With `--quiet` the tool prints the totals, the comparison of options and, for each total, only the ten effects of largest expected value and the ten of largest share of variance. The file it writes is the same.

3.5 What-if. `tools/what_if.py cases/<case> --set "NAME=LOW,MIDDLE,HIGH" [--set ...] [--skip LABEL ...] [--keep]` shows in a minute what a change of a few inputs does to a case's totals, the change a spreadsheet's user makes by typing a couple of numbers. In a scratch copy of the repository at HEAD it gives every block named NAME, in every `.toml` file of the case, the new low, middle and high, reruns the case's steps and prints each option's expected total before, after and the difference, in millions, with its share of draws above zero; the caller's tree is never changed. Its result is a lead and never a result of record: nothing it prints is cited, and a change of record goes through the case's author and its review. A case states its rerun steps in `cases/<case>/rerun.toml`: `totals`, the totals file's path under the case folder, and one `[[step]]` per step, each with a `label` and a `command` run as `PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python <command>` from the repository's root.

3.6 Assembling the table. The author builds `cases/<case>/estimates.toml` with `.venv/bin/python tools/estimate_total.py assemble cases/<case>`, run from the project root, and writes no code (roadmap item 2.33). It reads `cases/<case>/estimates/changes.toml`, which the author writes: `files`, the estimator files of `estimates/` in table order, and one `[[change]]` per change, each with its `reason`: `do = "replace"` of one key of a factor (`factor`, `key`, `value`), in every file that states it; `do = "replace"` of one key of a row (`effect`, optionally `file`, `key`, `value`); `do = "drop"` of an effect's rows (`effect`, optionally `file`); and `do = "merge"` of two effects into one (`effects = [A, B]`, `into = C`), each taking half the weight. The rows keep the order of `files` and of each file, which fixes the draws. A factor two or more files state is written once as a `[factors.<name>]` table. The command refuses, writing no table, a factor stated differently in two places (naming both files; the author resolves it by a replace of the factor), a change with no reason, a change naming an effect or factor no row states, and a file of `estimates/` with blocks that `files` does not list. The format in full is in the tool's opening lines.
