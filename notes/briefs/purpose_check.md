# Brief: purpose check of a case against the owner's rules, before its documents are written

A check dispatched as the shared `referee` agent type (`.claude/agents/referee.md`), with no definition of its own. It runs after a case's model is run or rerun and its author has drafted a one-page outline of what the paper will say (the headline, the ranking of the options, the three or four inputs that move the result furthest, the limits), and before any document is written or rewritten. The owner's yes [the owner's message of 2026-11-12]: "yes to 34.2, add to the handoff after the model is built (and possibly after a paper outline is drafted?) but before the work goes into writing it." The reviewers check a case against itself, that its numbers reproduce and its documents match the paper of record; this seat checks it against what he would ask on reading it. Whoever dispatches it, a coordinator or a workflow, fills three things and sends only the text below the marker line, with nothing added: `[[case folder]]`, such as `cases/<case>`; `[[outline]]`, the path of the author's outline; `[[report]]`, the path the report is written to, such as `cases/<case>/review/purpose_check.md`.

=== BRIEF ===

This is the purpose check of the case in [[case folder]], in this repository. Its model has just been run, its author has drafted a one-page outline of what the paper will say, and no document is written from these results yet. You check the case's results, its tables and that outline against the owner's own rules for a cost-benefit analysis and against the case's requirements, and say what he would object to on reading them, before the work of writing is spent. The case's other reviewers check it against itself.

1. Read:
1.1 the seat's summary of his rules (his post, imported at his request, the owner's message of 2026-10-29), numbered 1 to 17 under "How the owner does a cost-benefit analysis" in `notes/owner_cba_rules.md`. The literature for each is in research/cba_301_practical_advice.md (rules 1 to 13) and research/welfare_estimation_201.md (rules 14 to 17); open it where a rule's meaning is in doubt;
1.2 [[case folder]]/requirements.md, the user's requirements; CASE.md, the case's index; and review/points.toml, the record of review points, whose rows with a `limit` are the case's stated limits;
1.3 the tables of estimates, any model's inputs, and the results the run wrote, with every list of what is outside the total (the total tool refuses an effect left outside the total, so it prints none; CASE.md names any other);
1.4 [[outline]]: the headline, the ranking of the options, the three or four inputs that move the result furthest, and the limits. A part that is missing, or that the results do not bear out, is a finding.
Read past these only to trace a finding: design.md, the briefs the case's estimators were given or a ruling in review/, to learn where a rule or a change of structure came from; the pooled list of effects and the previous version or milestone, to learn what has left the total.

2. Simplicity. Complexity is slop: a good model is elegant, and a person can audit it by hand. Breadth is not complexity. Every effect people might raise gets a rough number, a product of a few factors anyone can check, and depth goes only to the few effects that move the result, each a model small enough for one spreadsheet tab; simplicity never means leaving an effect out. Any addition names the simpler choice it rejected, and why. The owner's reason (2026-11-10): "If you have six benefit streams, each one simple enough to explain in a one-paragraph example calculation, and any four of them will justify the policy even if two are zeroed out, this is vastly more credible than 'trust my thousand lines of code'." The literature agrees: "With independent alternative ways of deriving a result, the result is always surer than its weakest derivation", so long as the routes share no assumption (wimsatt_1981_robustness, pp. 66-67); "If the models are too complex to be assessed by well-informed nonexperts, then there is no way to know what biases they may contain or how plausible their results might be" (saltelli_2014_models); and across forecasting studies, "Complexity increases forecast error by 27 percent on average" (green_2015_simple, p. 2).

3. Rule by rule. Take the rules 1 to 17 in order, then each requirement of requirements.md by its number. For each write one line, one of three:
3.1 met, with the place where the case meets it: a file and line, a table or a result;
3.2 a departure, which is a finding (5);
3.3 not to be judged yet, because the documents are what meet it or the level requirements.md names does not reach it (.claude/skills/scope-case/presets.md 10.1 to 10.4), with what the documents must then carry.
Judge what the results, the tables and the outline hold, never an intention: a promise in the outline meets a rule only where the results hold what the paper would need to keep it.

4. What to look at hardest. A case can agree with itself and still miss each of these, so the reviewers who check it against itself cannot see them. For each, say what you looked at and what you found, or "none found" with where you looked.

4.1 Every uncertain quantity carries its whole range. An effect that may help or harm is one quantity whose draws take either sign; a chance stays below 1 by its form, never by a cap; each factor's form follows the quantity (`notes/factor_forms.md`); nothing uncertain is held at one value (rule 1). The practice: the ISPOR-SMDM good-practice report on parameter estimation and uncertainty (Briggs et al. 2012, `briggs_2012_model`) and OMB Circular A-4 on treating uncertainty (`omb_2023_circulara4`).

4.2 Every effect of size is in the total, its interval and the ranking of inputs. Compare the effects in the total with the pooled list of effects and with the previous version's or milestone's table, and name each effect that has left, with the range it last had beside the total's 90 per cent interval. An effect whose sign is unknown stays in the total as a quantity of either sign, and one with large draws is named as a target for research in the value-of-information ranking (Claxton 1999, `claxton_1999_irrelevance`; Fenwick et al. 2020, `fenwick_2020_value`).

4.3 Every rule of method that shapes the result has a source. Each rule that fixes a form, splits or joins effects, or caps or leaves something out cites the owner's words at the scope he said them, or a guidebook; a limit of a tool is fixed in the tool instead of written into the method. The practice: name the established technique and where the work departs from it (Boardman et al. 2018, `boardman_2018_cost`; Bojke et al. 2009 on structural uncertainty, `bojke_2009_characterizing`).

4.4 The results show what moves them. The results rank the inputs by how far each moves expected net benefits, the ranking a tornado diagram draws, and the outline's three or four inputs are the ones that ranking gives. The headline has its interval and its chance of being above zero; each option has its chance of leading; each input that drives the result is named as a target for research and elicitation; results are planned as tables and figures. The practice: Briggs et al. 2012 on presenting uncertainty and the CHEERS 2022 reporting standard (Husereau et al. 2022, `husereau_2022_cheers`).

4.5 The model keeps the form the requirements set: as few scenarios as they ask for, each defined apart; no larger than the version before unless a requirement asks for more; every structure a requirement serves; each effect's path short enough to audit by hand. Your definition's size check is done here. The practice: a model that well-informed non-experts can assess (Saltelli and Funtowicz 2014, `saltelli_2014_models`; Saltelli et al. 2020, `saltelli_2020_manifesto`).

4.6 Each judgement describes how the action works. A chance or a weight is one the decision-maker would recognise as a description of their own program; quantities one fact ties together are drawn together; the base case is the same world less the action (OMB Circular A-4 on the baseline, `omb_2023_circulara4`). A program that learns before it acts is valued through the decision its findings change (Claxton 1999).

4.7 Each design a reasonable person would consider is its own option. Ways of carrying out the policy are analysed side by side with a comparison, each its own run, instead of mixed into one draw as if they were an uncertainty; a design's size is chosen by a coarse search over the model, taking the lowest-cost size among those whose expected value is roughly the highest (the search and the lowest-cost rule are the seat's reading of the owner, 2026-11-12: "there will probably be a large range of values with roughly similar expected value, and we can aim for a program on the smaller end of that range, for cost and feasibility reasons"); a rule of choice reads one way only. The practice: the long list and short list of options in HM Treasury's Green Book (`hm_treasury_2026_green_book`) and OMB Circular A-4 on analysing alternatives.

5. Each finding, numbered PC1 onward and ranked by how far it could move the case's key result (expected net benefits, unless requirements.md names another), gives:
5.1 the rule or the requirement by its number, and the item of 4 where one fits;
5.2 what the case does, with the evidence as file:line and the line quoted;
5.3 its size in the case's own units: how far it could move the expected value, its interval or the ranking of the options, by arithmetic on the results' numbers, written out. A size that needs a rerun is given as a bound, with the run that would settle it; a finding that cannot be sized says why;
5.4 whose it is, one of three:
5.4.1 the author's to fix before writing: the case departs from a rule or does not do what a requirement says, the departure could reach the headline, its interval, the ranking of the options or the ranking of inputs, and correcting it needs no judgement of value;
5.4.2 a note for the Limits: a departure far inside the result's uncertainty, or one this level cannot mend. The author enters it as a row of the record of review points, `review/points.toml`, with `raised_by` "purpose check", writing its `limit` and `size`, and the row stays open until the editor rules on it;
5.4.3 the owner's to decide (for a requirement in a case with another user, that user's): a value judgement, a requirement or a reading of one, a rule of his and a requirement that pull apart, what the case is for. Mark it and decide nothing;
5.5 the fix, a deletion first; or the limit's sentence with its size; or the choice and what each answer changes, in plain words.

6. One verdict, the report's last line:
6.1 `VERDICT: write`: no finding is the author's, and no answer of the owner's could change the headline, the ranking of the options or what is in the total.
6.2 `VERDICT: write after these fixes`, naming the findings: at least one is the author's.
6.3 `VERDICT: stop for the owner`, naming the findings: his answer to at least one could change the headline, the ranking of the options or what is in the total, so that documents written now may be written twice. It wins over 6.2, and the author's fixes are still listed.

7. What you leave alone. You do not review prose: no document is under review, and the outline is judged for what it says leads, ranks, moves the result and limits it, not for its wording. You do not redo the audit: take the results as the run wrote them, trace no factor to its source, redo no estimate's arithmetic and rerun nothing. The check needs no web search. Your definition's questions serve the rules here, and this brief's form and verdict stand in place of its return.

8. Write your report to [[report]], change no other file and commit nothing. The report is this task's own file and stands over any general note against writing report files. Its order: the findings; the lines of 3; the items of 4; the verdict. Your return, under 15 lines before the WELFARE section: on its first line the verdict, exactly as in 6; the three gravest findings, one line each; how many findings are the author's, for the Limits and the owner's; the report's path.

Start by running `.venv/bin/python tools/purpose_flags.py [[case folder]] --previous <the previous version's git tag, from `git tag -n '<case>/v*'`, or its folder>` (leave out `--previous` for a first version). It prints three flags a script can see: model_size, a model larger than the version before (4.5); no_tornado, a paper with no tornado figure (4.4); one_sign, an effect netted against another or fixed to one sign since the version before (4.1). A flag is a place to look, never a finding by itself; what no flag shows, you look for under 4.
