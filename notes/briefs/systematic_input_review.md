# Systematic review of one input of a case: the brief

This brief is the method for replacing one input of a case with what a systematic review of its literature finds, the step the Full level adds after a normal draft. The case's author follows it end to end; the paper readers and the adjudicator take their rules from it through their definitions; and a methods reader checks it against PRISMA 2020 and the Cochrane Handbook. What belongs to one case alone (its input's quantity, its eligibility rules, search strings and known items, its quality domains, the scale it pools on) each review settles in its own protocol, and this brief names it only as the kind of thing meant.

## 1 The task

1.1 You are the author of an update of the case the task names, `cases/<case>/`, on `main`. Its current version has passed the middle tier (its `review/m1_editor.md` and `review/m2_editor.md`). Replace one input's distribution with what a systematic review finds, end to end. Every step must be present, and each may be crude. Later runs refine each step from this one: first the thinnest version that runs end to end (Cockburn 2004; CORE 6.3).
1.2 The owner asked for "doing a proper PRISMA lit search for the most important inputs" (the owner's message of 2026-11-07, 2026-11-07), and earlier named it one of the "optional steps (eg the PRISMA review of important variables after the first draft is done)" (the owner's message of 2026-11-05, 2026-11-05). For a major input, he said "yes to 10.5, do a proper meta analysis if theres enough data for it" (the owner's message of 2026-11-02, to the seat's option 10.5), and rule 9 of `notes/owner_cba_rules.md` has several studies of one kind combined by a random-effects meta-analysis, reporting the prediction for a new setting, where there are enough of them ("random-effects" and "reporting the prediction for a new setting" are the seat's wording).
1.3 The input is the one the task names, by its key in the case's `inputs.toml`; this brief calls its quantity θ. The protocol states what θ is and its unit, its present distribution and where that came from, and where the input ranks in the partial rank correlations with NPV in the case's results. Where the present distribution rests on one study, this brief calls it the anchor study.
1.4 How the model uses it. The protocol states where the model reads θ and what it computes from it. Where the model uses θ only through one quantity computed from it, this brief calls that quantity E. The review extracts θ itself, the quantity the studies report, and not what the model computes from it.
1.5 Version. Refining an input is a minor version (`notes/case_pipeline.md` 2.4.1). Record the commit you start from as the baseline.
1.6 Read first: `CLAUDE.md` ("How the owner does a cost-benefit analysis", "What a case delivers", "Where things live"); `notes/peer_review_trial.md` sections 1 to 4, the middle tier; the standing briefs in `notes/briefs/`, `author_upgrade.md` above all; `cases/README.md` (the input format); the headers of `research/bibliography.py` and `research/records.py`; and `cost_benefit_engine/README.md`.
1.7 The review steps of `notes/case_pipeline.md` section 5 and the process section of `research/README.md` point here.

## 2 Before the search: size the review, then store the methods sources

2.1 Size the review by its value of information (`notes/case_pipeline.md` 4.3: "sized to its value of information"). Where each draw's NPV is linear in E, the quantity the model computes from θ (1.4), so that NPV_i = a_i + b_i·E_i, and θ is drawn independently of every other input, the sizing is 2.1.1 to 2.4. Otherwise, read θ's first-order index from the main run's draws by a regression of NPV on θ (Strong and Oakley 2013, `strong_oakley_2013_efficient`, recalled, not stored), and predict the rerun's mean by reweighting the baseline draws by the new density of θ over the old (importance sampling, recalled). A sizing method other than these goes to the referee of 3.8 before the sizing runs.
2.1.1 Hold θ at two values far apart in its range, where E takes the values E_0 and E_1, with the engine's `run(case, model, iterations=1000, held={...})` on the case's seed, so both holds share every other draw (common random numbers). Compute each with the run script's headline, read as the run reports it. Then b_i = (NPV_i(E_1) − NPV_i(E_0)) / (E_1 − E_0) and a_i = NPV_i(E_0) − b_i·E_0.
2.1.2 Confirm the linearity first, on every path where E enters. On a few hundred draws, hold θ at a value between the two and check that NPV_i = a_i + E·b_i draw by draw, within rounding. If it fails, size the review by the regression of 2.1, and report which path breaks the linearity.
2.2 Report these as figures, not as a gate:
2.2.1 The swing E[b], the change in expected NPV at the headline rate per unit of E, and the swing across the current 5th to 95th percentiles of θ, E[b] times the difference in E between them.
2.2.2 For reference, 5% of the absolute value of the headline mean in the case's results. Auditing's standard gives five per cent of profit before tax as an example benchmark of materiality and fixes no percentage (ISA 320, paragraph A7, `iaasb_isa_320`).
2.2.3 The value-of-information reading: θ's first-order variance-based index, Var_θ(E[NPV | θ]) / Var(NPV), Oakley and O'Hagan's "main effect index" (2004, section 2.2). Where NPV is linear in E it equals E[b]²·Var(E) / Var(NPV), E being the model's function of θ. Beside it, the run's EVPPI for θ and its EVPI at the headline (the results' value-of-information section). That figure prices learning θ for the choice the case studies.
2.3 The full search always runs in this skeleton. These figures show what the review can move, and they size later reviews.
2.4 Before the full rerun of 8.2, predict its headline. Where NPV is linear in E: the baseline mean plus E[b] times the change in the mean of E from the old distribution of θ to the new, with the standard error from the 1,000 draws of b, the new mean of E taken from the new input's distribution of θ alone, by many draws or by quadrature. Otherwise: the reweighted mean of 2.1. The rerun is then compared with the prediction (8.3), an independent check (CORE 3.3).
2.5 Store the methods sources (CORE 2.2). Before the protocol cites a methods source not yet in `research/sources/<key>/`, store it there, both the original and a text extract. A `source-fetcher` agent (the shared `.claude/agents/source-fetcher.md`) can do this, given `research/refs.toml` as its index by your definition's item 6. Update each entry's status in `research/refs.toml` through the fetcher's fragment. A source with no free copy keeps an entry saying why.
2.6 The keys: page_2021_prisma, page_2021_prisma_ee, rethlefsen_2021_prisma_s, moher_2015_prisma_p, lefebvre_2025_cochrane_ch4, li_2019_cochrane_ch5, garritty_2024_updated, sampson_2006_alternative, higgins_2009_reevaluation, riley_2011_interpretation, rover_2021_weakly, rover_2020_bayesmeta, flemyng_2025_position, oakley_2004_probabilistic, strong_oakley_2013_efficient and iaasb_isa_320. Riley 2011 has no free copy, and Strong and Oakley 2013 is not stored.
2.7 If a stored text contradicts a choice in 6 to 8, keep the brief's choice and list the conflict in the report, for the reviewers of 10 to rule on.

## 3 The protocol, fixed before the search

3.1 Write `cases/<case>/reviews/<input>/protocol.md` from the PRISMA-P items that matter here (Moher et al. 2015, *Systematic Reviews* 4:1, doi:10.1186/2046-4053-4-1): the question, eligibility, information sources, search strings, selection, data items, risk of bias and synthesis. Commit it before any search. Under PRISMA 2020 item 24a, report it as "not registered; protocol committed at <hash, time> in a private repository". Every later change is appended as a dated amendment with its reason: "Document all post hoc changes" (Garritty et al. 2024).
3.2 The question, in one sentence of the protocol: across the studies that estimate θ, what is its value, and what value should be predicted for a further setting?
3.3 Eligibility.
3.3.1 Included: the protocol names the kinds of study that estimate θ for the case's question and what a study must report: θ, or a measure that converts exactly to θ (3.4).
3.3.2 Excluded: studies that only fit or reuse other studies' values, such as an anchor study, which are kept for the known items and for citation chasing. The protocol adds its own exclusions, each with its reason, for example forms of the intervention the case does not study (the case's `design.md`), or studies whose measure leaves out the part that drives most of what the model uses θ for (recorded but not pooled).
3.3.3 Works not in English are excluded, a stated restriction (Garritty et al. 2024 permits it, recalled). There is no limit on date.
3.4 The quantity, and how a study reporting another measure is handled. The protocol lists each conversion, which must be exact in the relation the model uses.
3.4.1 θ reported directly: taken as reported.
3.4.2 A measure from which θ follows by an exact formula: converted by that formula.
3.4.3 Every other measure is recorded with its value as "not convertible" and kept out of the pool.
3.4.4 One estimate per study, chosen in an order the protocol fixes over what a study measured and how. The definition each estimate uses is recorded; definitions are never averaged.
3.5 Sources.
3.5.1 The protocol names every database it searches (PRISMA-P item 9: "Describe all intended information sources"). The search tool covers three: OpenAlex, with `--limit` at least the reported hit count so every record is returned; Crossref, top 200 by relevance, since its search ranks and does not filter (a stated restriction); and PubMed. A review of a health input searches PubMed, and the lack of CENTRAL and Embase is a stated limit, since Cochrane's rule for its own reviews is "The minimum databases to be covered are ... CENTRAL, MEDLINE and Embase" (Lefebvre et al. 2025, MECIR C24).
3.5.2 The protocol names each index left out and why: PubMed where the question lies outside biomedicine; Semantic Scholar, Scopus, Web of Science and Google Scholar, since the search tool has no client for them. These are stated limits.
3.5.3 Citation chasing through `research/bibliography.py`: backward from the anchor study and from every included study, and forward from the work that defines θ, since a study that computes it should cite that work.
3.5.4 Browsing (PRISMA-S item 4): where the field keeps a list of its studies, browse it. Log its address, the date and the works taken from it, and mark its address and completeness as unchecked. Enter each work by `lookup` of its DOI, found by `browse:<list>`, and count it by hand in the other-methods column, since `records.prisma` counts only searches and chases.
3.6 Known items, for relative recall (Sampson et al. 2006): studies known to be eligible before the search. Where an anchor study cites them, recall is counted on the database records only, before any chase. Try to fetch and store the anchor study's supplementary material where that holds its fit.
3.7 Search strings. Draft them for OpenAlex's boolean `search`, which stems words itself, so it has no wildcards: blocks joined by AND, the terms inside a block joined by OR, for example three blocks: the names the intervention goes by, the words for the comparison the measure makes, and the variables. Crossref takes the plain words, and PubMed its own field tags. The protocol states the final strings exactly as run (PRISMA-S item 8).
3.8 Departures from this brief. The strings are checked by the recall of known items (4.2), not by PRESS peer review, a stated departure from recommendation 6 of Garritty et al. 2024. Any other method choice the protocol makes beyond this brief goes to the referee before the search (`notes/what_a_case_delivers.md`), and a sizing method beyond 2.1 before the sizing runs. That referee, like every seat but the search step, makes no live query of the review's databases: a referee's probes of an index can draw a 429, and the cooldown rule then closes that index for a day.

## 4 Search and duplicates

4.1 Run each string with `.venv/bin/python research/bibliography.py search --database D --log <review>/search_log.jsonl`. Load the records into `<review>/records.jsonl` with `records.add`, which merges duplicates by DOI, then by title and year. Log each chase in `<review>/chase_log.jsonl`: the seed DOI, the direction, the date and the records returned. Never put a chase in the search log, which `records.prisma` counts by database.
4.2 After the database searches and before any chase, check which known items are in the records. If one is missing, revise the strings once as a logged amendment and run again. Report recall on the database records alone.
4.3 Cost guard. If more than 2,000 records remain after duplicates are merged, tighten the strings as a logged amendment, checking that every known item the databases found still comes back.

## 5 Screening, full text and extraction

5.1 Title and abstract.
5.1.1 Two screeners, one Sonnet and one Haiku, each a `paper-reader` agent (`.claude/agents/paper-reader.md`, which holds the reading rules and the welfare paragraph) with its model and effort set in the call and named in its task, each screen every record in batches of about 50. The known items go in the first batch. Each screener sees only the protocol's eligibility section and its batch: title, abstract, venue and year, and no citation count.
5.1.2 Each screener is a separate agent call that writes only its own file (`screening_sonnet.jsonl`, `screening_haiku.jsonl`). Each is told not to open any other file of the review, and neither sees the other's output.
5.1.3 A third seat, a fresh `adjudicator` agent (`.claude/agents/adjudicator.md`), decides only the disagreements, seeing both decisions and their reasons. A disagreement is one screener excluding where the other includes or is unsure. Where neither excludes, the record goes forward.
5.1.4 Only Anthropic models screen. Where the case is a client's, free models never see client material (`CLAUDE.md`, "Where things live"). Free models are also the less reliable readers on judgement.
5.2 Why two screeners, and how they are checked.
5.2.1 Cochrane's conduct rule is "at least two people working independently" (Lefebvre et al. 2025, MECIR C39).
5.2.2 Two model instances share errors, so their agreement shows shared judgement, not accuracy. Cohen's kappa is therefore reported and gates nothing.
5.2.3 The checks against reality are the recall of known items (4.2) and each screener's decision on each known item. Report those decisions as the pilot of the screening tools: synthesists "may need to pilot (or calibrate) the AI system or tool to validate its performance" (Flemyng et al. 2025).
5.2.4 A single model screener over-includes (Tran et al. 2024: sensitivity 94.6% to 99.8%, specificity 2.2% to 46.6%). The full-text step absorbs that.
5.3 Recording the decisions. A script imports both screeners' decisions with `records.decide`, under the seats `screener-sonnet` and `screener-haiku`. It then writes the final decision for every record last: "both agreed", or the third seat's decision. `records.prisma` reads the last decision.
5.4 Full text.
5.4.1 Fetch open copies politely, by `source-fetcher` agents given the case's `sources.toml` as their index by your definition's item 6. Store every full text fetched, included or not, in `cases/<case>/sources/<key>/` in the case's form: the original plus `text.txt`, keyed author_year. Merge its `sources.toml` entry, with citation, licence, DOI and URL, from the fetcher's fragment before it is cited.
5.4.2 Mark a work that cannot be had with `records.mark_not_retrieved` and its reason. List it for the owner, who is not asked to fetch it. Check each included work for retraction by its OpenAlex `is_retracted` field, and record the result.
5.5 Eligibility and extraction at full text. No expensive model reads a paper whole. The owner [the owner's message of 2026-11-08]: "make a plan that does not require an expensive model to do a full text read of papers. Surely you can come up with a process where cheap readers look over things, are encouraged to err on the side of inclusion to avoid missing things, and expensive models only read excerpts or adjudicate disagreements."
5.5.1 Three `paper-reader` agents, one paper a seat, model and effort set in the call, each read every retrieved text to its end, blind to the others. Readers A and B, Haiku at medium effort, record every value of θ, of a measure that converts to it (3.4) or of its parts, and every pointer to material not stored, each with its quote; A answers unsure unless the text plainly fails a criterion. Reader S, Sonnet at medium effort, confirms eligibility and extracts in one read (Li et al. 2019, chapter 5, recalled): an excluded work gets its first failed criterion; otherwise the measure, its value, any uncertainty reported and the fields of 6.2, quoted by the definition. S answers unsure, never no, where the text does not settle a criterion, where a pointer names a measure not stored, or where the paper prints the parts from which θ converts exactly (3.4.2).
5.5.2 A script sends a paper to the adjudicator, once every quote is checked (5.6.1), where S answers yes or unsure; or S answers no and any reader recorded, with a quote that is found, a value convertible to θ or its parts; or S fails it on whether θ is reported and the text matches the script's own pattern for θ's measures; or S's form is missing, invalid or unfinished after one rerun. Every other paper is excluded on S's first failed criterion. A's unsure alone sends nothing up. A text that is only an abstract goes up only where a reader recorded a value; otherwise S's no excludes it and any other answer makes it unresolved.
5.5.3 The adjudicator, a fresh `adjudicator` agent, rules from a pack the script builds of the passages holding each reader's quotes, values and pointers, their neighbours and the captions, with all three forms, to at most 40% of the text. It may open further chunks or pages while the share it read stays at most one half (characters of passages read over the text's, plus pages opened over the original's), which the script counts from its transcript; a ruling past the cap is set aside as unresolved. It rules `include`, `exclude` with the first failed criterion, `awaiting supplement` (the only open question is a pointer to material not stored) or `unresolved` (the excerpts do not settle it), naming what is left open. The last two await classification (Lefebvre et al. 2025, 4.6.4). For an included paper the adjudicator's checked value is the first extraction and S's form the second (MECIR C46).
5.5.4 The report states two departures from MECIR C39: an exclusion by the adjudicator on whether θ is reported rests on excerpts, not "the full texts"; and an exclusion by S after A's unsure rests on one judge, not two.
5.5.5 Before the run, the readers are calibrated on texts with known answers, since data forms "should be thoroughly pilot-tested before launch" (Li et al. 2019, 5.4.3): real texts with reference reads, and copies with planted values, no seat told which. The bar and its scorer are fixed in the protocol before any seat runs, and the scorer must first pass on the reference reads themselves. Values that convert to no estimate of θ gate nothing, and the report gives their recall. The bar does not require keeping a known item the reference read excludes. If readers A and B fail, the next arrangement is tried with fresh seats on the same texts and bar: both on Haiku at high effort, then B on Sonnet, then S counted as a recorder on the planted copies, then stop and report. The report gives each arrangement's score.
5.6 Quotes and conversions.
5.6.1 Check every quote with `records.quote_found(key, quote, root="cases/<case>/sources")`.
5.6.2 A number read off a figure is recorded as "read from figure N by <method>", marked as an inference (CORE 3.1) and rated "some concern" in 6.2.4.
5.6.3 A script, not an extractor, converts each measure to θ by 3.4 and writes `estimates.csv`.

## 6 Quality of the studies

6.1 A study of a kind a standard tool fits is rated by that tool, chosen in the protocol (3.8): RoB 2 for randomised trials, ROBINS-I for non-randomised studies of interventions, ROBINS-E for studies of exposures, QUADAS-2 for studies of test accuracy (all recalled, not stored). A kind of study no validated tool fits is a gap stated for the referee.
6.2 Where no standard tool fits, the protocol writes a few domains specific to the case. Each is rated low, some or high concern, by rules written in the protocol, from the fields the extractors record. For example:
6.2.1 Indirectness, how far the study's version of the intervention is from the one the case studies: the version the case studies is low; a cruder version is some; an idealisation is some, with the direction it likely biases θ stated.
6.2.2 What the study measured and how, as in 3.4.4.
6.2.3 Signal against noise: a sample too small to tell them apart is some concern.
6.2.4 The conversion: direct or exact is low; read from a figure is some.
6.2.5 Departure from the case's range: an intervention far larger than the case's is some concern.
6.3 Ratings do not weight the pool. One sensitivity analysis keeps only the studies rated low on indirectness (6.2.1) and on any domain the protocol names for it. No GRADE rating of certainty is made, a gap stated under PRISMA items 15 and 22.
6.4 Reporting bias is not assessed. With few estimates, funnel tests do not work. PRISMA items 14 and 21 are answered so. The MAER-Net guidelines (Havránek et al. 2020) are for economic effect sizes and are not used.

## 7 Synthesis

7.1 Dependence, at least crudely. The protocol says what one study is and what a report of it is. PRISMA 2020 counts reports and studies apart (Page et al. 2021).
7.1.1 One estimate per study, read from its tables, or from its figures under 5.6.2. Several reports of one study give one estimate, chosen by the order of 3.4.4.
7.1.2 A study measured under several designs, arms or experiments gives the main pool the one nearest the case's question, by 6.2.1.
7.1.3 Studies that share a sample, code or team form a family, and enter the main analysis one each, since studies that share their machinery tend to err alike. A sensitivity analysis keeps one per family, the median on the pooling scale.
7.2 Enough to pool means three or more distinct studies after 7.1. Below three, the prior sets most of the spread across studies, and Higgins et al.'s t-interval with k − 2 degrees of freedom does not exist.
7.3 The estimator, used when there are three or more.
7.3.1 The normal-normal hierarchical model on y, θ on the pooling scale the protocol states (log for a ratio or a positive quantity, logit for a proportion, identity for a difference): y_i ~ N(μ, τ² + s_i²), with a flat prior on μ and a half-normal prior on τ.
7.3.2 The posterior is computed by numerical integration over a grid of τ, as `bayesmeta` computes it (Röver 2020). The input is the posterior predictive distribution of y for a new setting, mixed over τ's posterior (Higgins, Thompson and Spiegelhalter 2009; Riley et al. 2011).
7.4 Why it is the small-sample choice. With few studies, plug-in estimates of τ (DerSimonian-Laird, REML) understate uncertainty: "standard approaches to inference tend to perform poorly" (Röver et al. 2021). The Bayesian predictive carries τ's uncertainty into the interval. It also works where a study reports no within-study error, which leaves frequentist weights undefined.
7.5 The prior. The half-normal family is weakly informative (Röver et al. 2021). The protocol sets its scale so that τ's 95th percentile gives a range for a new study's θ that is extreme and unlikely on the pooling scale, as Röver et al. do (their 3.4.3: considering such a range "extreme and unlikely, a half-Normal prior with scale 0.18 ... was eventually suggested for a log-RR"). On the log scale, a scale of 0.5 puts 95% of τ below 0.98, and at τ = 0.98, 95% of studies' θ would lie within a factor of about 7 either side of the median. The predictive is also reported at half and at double the chosen scale.
7.6 Within-study error. s_i comes from the study's own report of its error, by the delta method on the pooling scale (on the log scale, s = SE(θ)/θ). Where a study gives a confidence interval or a p-value instead, the standard error is derived from it (Cochrane Handbook 6.5.2, recalled, not stored). Where it gives nothing, s_i = 0, so all the spread between studies is read as heterogeneity. That widens the predictive, the conservative side.
7.7 The predictive treats what the case needs as exchangeable with the studies. The studies may share an error that sets them all apart from the case's setting; this is a stated limit, and that gap is named as a quantity for the case's expert survey.
7.8 Checks, run in the script and not committed as tests, under the test gate: with τ held near zero, the posterior mean equals the inverse-variance weighted mean; and the script reproduces the worked example of Röver 2020 (its section 3).
7.9 Fewer than three. Keep the present distribution and report the estimates beside it. If an estimate falls outside its 5th to 95th percentiles, widen it to span the estimate. The evidence type becomes `several_sources`, and the reason goes in the note. With no usable study, the input is unchanged and the report says so.
7.10 This meta-analysis is one method. A second, independent method for θ, such as the case's planned expert survey, lies outside this skeleton (rule 9), a stated limit.

## 8 The new input and the rerun

8.1 The new entry.
8.1.1 Form: `distribution = "metalog"` through the predictive's 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 95th percentiles in θ's unit, with `lower_bound = 0` for a quantity that cannot be negative. Where the model caps θ, it caps it, and the report gives the predictive's share beyond the cap. If the engine refuses the fit, use the engine's form that is normal on the pooling scale (a lognormal on the log scale) through the predictive's median and its spread from the 5th to the 95th percentile, and say so.
8.1.2 Evidence type `meta_analysis`, with `sources` (the pooled studies) and one verbatim `quote` and `pinpoint` for each, in order. The note points to `report.md` and `meta_analysis.json`. The anchor study stays cited for what the model still takes from it.
8.2 Rerun the case from the root with its run script as a module (`.venv/bin/python -m cases.<case>.<run script>`), in the background where the run is long. The loader checks every quote against its stored text. Refill the documents with `documents.py`, and rewrite the technical appendix's paragraphs on the input to describe the review.
8.3 Report the change, filled by script from the results file at the baseline and at the new run, never typed: the headline mean, 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles and P(NPV > 0), at the headline rate and at each other rate the case reports; the headline against its prediction from 2.4; the new θ's 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles beside the present distribution's, and E's mean and median under each; and the input's partial rank correlation and its EVPPI.

## 9 The report and its checks

9.1 The flow diagram and counts.
9.1.1 Take the counts from `records.prisma`, and split them by hand into PRISMA 2020's two columns: databases (the logged searches) and other methods (chasing and browsing). A record found both ways counts under databases. The template is "separated by source" (Page et al. 2021b).
9.1.2 Give studies and reports apart (7.1). Draw `flow.md` as a table and a Mermaid chart.
9.1.3 Before drawing, check in each column that: identified less removed equals screened; screened less excluded equals sought; sought less not retrieved equals assessed; assessed less excluded and awaiting classification (5.5.3) equals included. A mismatch stops the review: these are accounting identities (CORE 3.3).
9.2 `report.md` holds:
9.2.1 Each PRISMA 2020 item (Page et al. 2021) and each of PRISMA-S's 16 (Rethlefsen et al. 2021), answered or marked "not done in this skeleton".
9.2.2 Item 25, support: who commissioned the analysis (`CASE.md` 1.1). Item 26, competing interests, stated as they are, any stake of the commissioner in the research appraised among them.
9.2.3 The search record: the strings "copied and pasted exactly as run", with dates and hit counts; the known-item recall on database records, and each screener's decision on each known item; kappa; the share of included studies by route; retractions; and the works excluded at full text, with reasons.
9.2.4 The results: the estimates table, grouped by the definition of θ, with its conversions and ratings; the synthesis and its sensitivities (prior scale, one per family, low indirectness only); the derivation of the input; and the limits.
9.3 Use of AI. Cochrane, Campbell, JBI and the Collaboration for Environmental Evidence state that "Any use of AI or automation that makes or suggests judgements should be fully and transparently reported in the evidence synthesis report", and that AI "should be used with human oversight" (Flemyng et al. 2025).
9.3.1 The report names every model and its role. It keeps each seat's prompt verbatim in `prompts/`, and gives the pilot of 5.2.3. It says that no person screened or extracted, and that oversight comes after the run, through the packet of 9.4.
9.4 The analysts' audit packet.
9.4.1 `analyst_packet.csv` holds 25 title-and-abstract decisions drawn at random from the exclusions and 25 from the records sent forward, with the seed stated (all of them where there are fewer), plus every screener disagreement, and at full text every exclusion, every include and every report awaiting classification, with the exclusions by reader S alone marked as the class to sample first. Each row gives the record, the stage, the decision, its reason and its seat. Two empty columns are left for the auditor, `agree` and `note`.
9.4.2 `analyst_packet.md` explains the question, the eligibility rules and how to mark, for someone who has opened nothing else. If none of a stratum's 25 is marked wrong, its error rate is under about 11% at 95% confidence (1 − 0.05^(1/25)).

## 10 Review of the new content
