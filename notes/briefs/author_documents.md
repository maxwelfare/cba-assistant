The coordinator fills each `[[...]]`; a message is the text from its marker line to the next, and no marker line is sent.

Why it exists. A one-page summary written with no brief comes out worse than one written to a form. The page that works draws every number from a generated facts file and is rebuilt and looked at after each edit; the page that fails is columns of text with no box, chart, tile or table, its headline buried. Items A.2, A.4 and A.7 hold the form.

How the prose reads. The owner, 2026-11-12 (the owner's message of 2026-11-12): "I personally do not like it when the text of the paper repeats things that are in tables and graphs, and prefer text to be more of a teaching narrative explaining the structure and giving understanding and context to the result values, eg lots of example calculations." The field agrees on the first half (icmje_2026_recs: "Do not repeat all the data in the tables or figures in the text") and, for readers who are not specialists, on the second (the worked-example effect, sweller_1985_worked, whose abstract reports later problems of the same structure solved faster after worked examples; narrative gives "increased comprehension, interest, and engagement", dahlstrom_2014_narratives, its abstract), with two refinements that are the seat's, not his: the answer comes first (idrc_2026_policy_brief: the summary "is the first thing a reader will see"), and the few headline numbers are repeated on purpose (the same guide: the conclusion is "a mirror to your introduction"). Item A.5.1 is the rule.
