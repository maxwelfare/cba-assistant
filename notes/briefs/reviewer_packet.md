What it serves. The owner: "the whole point of this project is to run overnight and get a draft suitable for human peer review, expert elicitation, and auditing", and part of the deliverable is "making packets for peer reviewers, explaining the overall process and asking for them to audit their expertise" (the owner's message of 2026-11-02). A peer review package is the bundle sent to a reviewer: "the draft work product, charge and other pertinent material", where a charge is "a set of focused questions that identifies the scientific and technical issues on which the Agency would like feedback" (EPA Peer Review Handbook, 4th ed., 2015, sections 6.2.5.1 and 6.2.1; `epa_2015_peer_review_handbook`). The charge to reviewers takes the form of the US bulletin on peer review (`omb_2004_peer_review_bulletin`), with a journal's guidance to its reviewers for what the reviewer is asked to declare (`plos_2026_reviewer_guidelines`). Three choices of this brief, each the session's and not the owner's words:

1. The author writes the peer review packages. It knows which parts of the work rest on which field, and in the bulletin the body that made an assessment drafts the charge to its reviewers ("In drafting the charge", "Scope of the Review"). So that the charge does not steer a reviewer away from the work's weak points, every point the review left open and every stated limit goes into some peer review package (its 2.3).
2. His words "asking for them to audit their expertise" are read as asking each reviewer to audit the parts of the analysis in their field, and to say for each part whether it lies within their expertise and whether they checked it (its 3.6). The second half also covers the other reading, a reviewer rating their own competence, at the cost of one column of the reply sheet.
3. A peer review package is written for one reviewer and named by the field, never by the person. The bulletin has the charge "determined in advance of the selection of the reviewers" ("Scope of the Review"); the user picks the people, and no reviewer's personal data enters a file of the case that might be shared (`notes/case_pipeline.md` 6.6, the same rule for experts). A peer review package is written in the language `requirements.md` records for that reviewer's field; its English working copy is kept beside it.

Not here. The analysts' packet for a systematic review's screening decisions is made by that review's own brief (`notes/briefs/systematic_input_review.md` 9.4); a peer review package names it where it exists. Taking the reviewers' replies back into the analysis is not built (roadmap R19).

Cost, by estimate and not measured: about $1.1 to $3.4 for two to four peer review packages (`.claude/agents/case-author.md` 3.6 says how), since the author reads little that it has not read already; and for each reviewer, the half-day of message 3.3.

=== BRIEF ===

The analysis is accepted at [[the milestone, such as milestone 1]]: please make its peer review packages for human peer reviewers. The owner asked for "packets for peer reviewers, explaining the overall process and asking for them to audit their expertise" (the owner's message of 2026-11-02). A peer review package goes to one person who is expert in one field. It tells them how the analysis was made, points them to the parts of it in their field, and asks them to check those parts and to say where their expertise ends. The bulletin on peer review describes the form: a charge is "most powerful when the charge is specific and steers the reviewers to specific technical questions while also directing reviewers to offer a broad evaluation of the overall product" (omb_2004_peer_review_bulletin, "Scope of the Review").

1. Read again what you need: documents/technical_appendix.md, above all its table of effects with each one's share of the total's variance, and the three tables of its record of choices and review (author_upgrade.md, message A, 2.1); requirements.md; and the record of review points, review/points.toml, which the last response letter in review/ prints as its comment resolution matrix.

2. The fields, and how many peer review packages.
2.1 Where requirements.md names reviewers or the fields the user wants reviewed, make one peer review package for each reviewer named, in that reviewer's field, or one for each field named.
2.2 Otherwise, make one peer review package for each field the analysis needs. These are the economics of the appraisal as a whole: its frame, discounting, valuation, transfers and double counting. They are the field of each effect that carries a material share of the headline, by the total tool's shares of the total's variance, such as epidemiology for a health effect or building engineering for the cost of a building's equipment. And they include statistics, where the model rests on a method a reviewer of the domain would not check, such as a meta-analysis or a simulation. Merge fields one person would cover. Make no peer review package for a part no input or result rests on. Two to four peer review packages is usual.
2.3 Every effect among the five with the largest shares of the total's variance goes into at least one peer review package. So does every point of the record of review that was declined, revised otherwise than asked, still disputed or left open, and every note in the Limits. These are where the work is weakest, and a peer review package that leaves them out steers its reviewer away from them.

3. Each peer review package is `cases/[[case]]/review/packets/<code>.md`, `<code>` a short name of the field such as `econ` or `epi`. Write it in plain words for a reader who knows their field and has opened nothing of this project, explaining each of the project's terms where it is first used. Keep it as short as it can be while it stays complete. Its parts, in this order:
3.1 The request, in a few lines. Give what the analysis asks: the question, the options, the base case, and the headline with its 90 per cent interval, as the appendix states them. Say what the reviewer is asked to do, and roughly how long it takes. Give the date to reply by: [[the date, or "to be set by the user"]]. Say what the reviewer is not asked to do: to say whether the policy should be adopted, or which value judgement to take where the user has taken one, such as the discount rate or whose gains count, since "the charge should make clear that the reviewers are not to provide advice on the policy" (the bulletin, the same section). The reviewer may still say whether a value judgement is stated and its effect shown.
3.2 How the analysis was made, honestly and in about a page.
3.2.1 Say that AI agents made it, and name the models: [[each seat that ran, with its model: the coordinator fills it from its own dispatches of the seats of `notes/fast_estimate.md` 1.3, each sent with a named model and, where a systematic review ran, from its `seats.jsonl`]].
3.2.2 Say that a user set its requirements in a conversation, and which requirements bear on this reviewer's parts.
3.2.3 Say that one agent wrote the estimates, the models and the documents, and that the other agents reviewed them. Give each round of review in one line: who reviewed, what they checked, how many points they raised, and how the points were ruled. Say that every point was answered in a table, and that the appendix lists each point not taken as its reviewer asked; after a fast estimate, say instead that the draft has not been revised and that the editor's points stand open.
3.2.4 Say that three blind agents, from one angle each, listed every effect they could, and that each was estimated by hand, the largest given small models.
3.2.5 Say whether any person has checked any part. Until a person has, say that none has, and that the reviewer is the first person to check the parts in their peer review package.
3.2.6 Name the parts that no reviewer, agent or person, has checked, such as a fix made after the referees' second round, which only the editor checked.
3.2.7 Where the case had a systematic review, say that its screening decisions have their own packet for analysts, by its path.
3.3 The parts in the reviewer's field, as a numbered table, one row per item, the items that most move the headline first. An item is an effect's estimate or one of its factors, a model's input, equation or function, a method choice from the first table of the appendix's record, a point of review left open, or a limit. Each row gives the item; where it is (the appendix's section, the name in estimates.toml or a model's file, the source's key and its stored file); what the analysis did, in a sentence with the value and its range; and the question for this reviewer. Keep the items to what a reviewer in the field can check in about half a day, and list any others after them as optional. The half-day is this process's judgement, not a source's.
3.4 The questions asked of every item, numbered so that a reply can cite them:
3.4.1 As far as your field can tell, is it right: the method, the value and the range?
3.4.2 Is the source the right one, and read rightly? Is a study the analysis should have used missing? The journal asks: "Are the claims properly placed in the context of the previous literature? Have the authors treated the literature fairly?" (plos_2026_reviewer_guidelines).
3.4.3 Is each value that is a judgement, not a fact, marked as one, and is the judgement reasonable? Reviewers help by "distinguishing scientific facts from professional judgments", and may be asked "to provide advice on the reasonableness of judgments made from the scientific evidence" (the bulletin, "Scope of the Review").
3.4.4 Is its uncertainty stated and wide enough, and is what it does to the result clear? "The charge should ask that peer reviewers ensure that scientific uncertainties are clearly identified and characterized" (the same).
3.4.5 What further research would most change the result? The bulletin suggests asking reviewers to consider "value-of-information analyses that identify whether more research is likely to decrease key uncertainties" (the same). Give the appendix's value of information or sensitivity result for the item where it has one.
3.5 The broad evaluation: anything in the whole analysis that the reviewer's field shows to be wrong or missing beyond the items, such as an effect it does not count.
3.6 The audit of expertise. For each item the reviewer says whether it lies within their expertise (yes, partly or no) and whether they checked it. At the end they say what they did not check and what they could not judge. The bulletin asks each reviewer for a report "that describes the nature and scope of their review and their findings and conclusions" (its account of Section III(6)), and the journal asks a reviewer to "Accept an invitation only if you have the knowledge, time and objectivity necessary to provide an unbiased assessment of the research". An item that no reviewer was competent to check is then known.
3.7 Competing interests and independence. Ask the reviewer to declare any interest in the policy, the user or those it affects, using the journal's examples: a recent or current collaboration with any of the authors, here the user; direct competition; or an opportunity to profit financially from the work. Ask whether they took part in making the analysis, since "Peer reviewers shall not have participated in development of the work product" (the bulletin, Section II(3)(c)).
3.8 What becomes of the reply. "Reviewers shall be notified in advance regarding the extent of disclosure and attribution planned" (the bulletin, Section II(5)). State it from requirements.md: [[the user's requirement on publishing the review and naming its reviewers, or "none"]]. Where requirements.md says nothing, the peer review package says that the reply is kept in the analysis's record of review without the reviewer's name. It asks whether the reviewer agrees to be named, and to a short paragraph on their credentials being published beside the review, which the bulletin's reports carry (Section III(6)). Where requirements.md does not say the analysis may be shared, the peer review package asks the reviewer to keep it and its files confidential, as the journal asks: "Keep manuscripts and correspondence confidential and do not share information about submissions with any one else unless previously agreed with the editor". Each default is a point for the user (its 6.4). The default of an unnamed reviewer departs from the bulletin, under which "The agency shall disclose the names of the reviewers and their organizational affiliations in the report" (Section II(5)); it is the conservative choice, since naming a person is the user's to decide with that person, and the user changes it in requirements.md.
3.9 How to reply: fill in the reply sheet of 4, and add any comments of their own beneath it, numbered from 1.
3.10 The files to send with it. These are the technical appendix, with the policy paper and the one-page summary where they exist; for its items, estimates.toml and the stored sources they cite; and, where the field reads code, the models and their results, since reviewers are to have "access to key studies, data and models" (the bulletin, its account of Section III(4)). Name each by its path. Whether the user sends the files or gives access to the repository is the user's to decide.
