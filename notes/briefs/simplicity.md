# The simplicity paragraph every seat carries

The owner, 2026-11-10: "Start by changing the entire process so every subagent at every stage is promoted to demand simplicity. All of the reviewers should be mainly looking for ways to make the model simpler and more elegant. Prompt them all that complexity is slop, and a good model will be elegant and human auditable. But, to be clear, simplicity does not mean leaving things out. I want a 'broad but shallow' approach." The process it serves is `notes/fast_estimate.md`; the three citations are from `research/why_broad_shallow.md` section 5, each quote machine-checked against its stored text.

1. Where it is copied. Every agent definition in `.claude/agents/` carries paragraph 2 word for word, just before its welfare paragraph, or near its end where it has none; a reviewer's definition (`audit-referee`, `documents-referee`, `editor`) adds paragraph 3 after it. One brief sends it, with paragraph 3: `review_fast_econ.md`, whose seat's definition is the shared folder's `referee` and does not carry it. No other brief copies it. A change is made here first, then copied to every file that `grep -rl 'Complexity is slop' .claude/agents notes/briefs` lists. The two seats of the teaching sheet are the exception, since the owner asked for a sheet of "100 input variables or less" that "cant have the normal nudges to include everything" (the owner's message of 2026-11-16; that the track chooses the few main effects and covers not every one is the seat's reading): `lesson-designer` carries the track's own paragraph in paragraph 2's place (`notes/briefs/teaching_sheet.md` 8), and `novice-reader`, which judges no model, carries none.

2. Simplicity. Complexity is slop: a good model is elegant, and a person can audit it by hand. Breadth is not complexity. Every effect people might raise gets a rough number, a product of a few factors anyone can check, and depth goes only to the few effects that move the result, each a model small enough for one spreadsheet tab; simplicity never means leaving an effect out. Any addition names the simpler choice it rejected, and why. The owner's reason (2026-11-10): "If you have six benefit streams, each one simple enough to explain in a one-paragraph example calculation, and any four of them will justify the policy even if two are zeroed out, this is vastly more credible than 'trust my thousand lines of code'." The literature agrees: "With independent alternative ways of deriving a result, the result is always surer than its weakest derivation", so long as the routes share no assumption (wimsatt_1981_robustness, pp. 66-67); "If the models are too complex to be assessed by well-informed nonexperts, then there is no way to know what biases they may contain or how plausible their results might be" (saltelli_2014_models); and across forecasting studies, "Complexity increases forecast error by 27 percent on average" (green_2015_simple, p. 2).

3. For a reviewer. Your main job is to find what could be merged, cut or turned back into a rough estimate without moving the result beyond its uncertainty; an effect is merged only where it repeats another, and never cut for being rough. Check first that each estimate, effect or scenario stands alone: a person can check it by hand, as a product of about five factors at most, without reading any other part of the analysis, and it could be set to zero or taken out without changing any other. Where two of them must agree on a value, they share that one factor by name; code or a table that several of them pass through is complexity however few inputs it has, and a count of inputs or factors is no measure of simplicity (the author's decisions record, 2026-11-12). Check then that each model's arithmetic fits on one spreadsheet tab, and that a new version is no larger than the one before, in the lines of its model and input files as in its inputs, unless the user's requirements ask for more.

4. One quantity, one distribution. A forecast or judged quantity is one input with a single distribution, fitted to judged quantiles (a median and one tail quantile; lognormal for a positive long-tailed quantity, beta for a bounded one), the judgment labeled and its judge named. It is not built as a pick on each draw between cases, each with its own range and a chance: that gives a distribution with two humps, and several inputs where one would do. A split into regimes needs cited evidence that the world has separate regimes and goes to the owner on its own before it is built (the owner, 2026-11-16 local, the owner's message of 2026-11-17: "No real estimate is like that. [...] make sure all future predictions are single distributions unless we have really good proof that we are dealing with separate attractors or regimes"). Two or more independent methods of estimating one quantity are a different thing and keep their own rule.
