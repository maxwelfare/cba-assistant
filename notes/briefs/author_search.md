# The case author's Method 3.0 to 3.7: data, the search for an input's sources, and independent methods

Read by the `case-author` agent before its first search of a case, and again after a compaction; never sent as a message. The numbers are its definition's Method numbers. `<case>` is the case's folder name.

3.0 Data before papers. A published figure is often a public dataset's number, older or for another place. Before you search the literature for an input, ask whether a dataset can give it directly, in this setting: shared/library/access.toml holds some 200 sources an analyst can fetch (IHME, the American Community Survey, statistics offices), each with what it holds, whether its numbers are measured or a publisher's model, how to reach it and on what terms. Send a `quick-reader` helper your list of inputs and the file's menu, `grep -nE '^\[|^holds' shared/library/access.toml`, to name the rows that could give each; read those rows yourself. A direct figure can be one of 3.7's independent methods. A source not listed that you use gets a table in that file. Why: decisions/how-an-author-searches-for-sources.md, attempt 3.
3.0.1 Store what you fetch as you would a paper, in sources/<key>/: the address or query, the date, and the response or a small extract of it as text.txt, so that an input's quote is the line holding the number. Take only what the input needs, politely (the shared `.claude/agents/source-fetcher.md`, How to think 5 and 6), and put nothing of a client's into a query.
3.0.2 Read the row's data_kind. A modelled estimate, such as IHME's, is built on studies and records you may also cite, so by 3.7.3.2 it may be one method with them.
3.0.3 A row whose credential is not `none`. Only a person makes an account, agrees to terms or requests a key; you never do. Which the user holds, and the rows each opens, `cd shared && ../.venv/bin/python -m tools.access status` prints, never a value. Where the row's env_var is set, use the source: the shared polite fetch and `tools.source_store` send the key themselves where the row names its `key_host`, and elsewhere a script of yours reads it by the shared `tools.access.value`, as `research/bibliography.py` does; never print a key or write it to any file. Where it is not set, or the row needs an application or payment, do not stop and do not ask: build the input from what you can reach, and add to cases/<case>/data_wanted.md one entry naming the source, the input, what it would add over what you used, and what going without it costs the result. Each time you are resumed, check whether a key wanted there has since been set, and use the source if so.
3.0.4 Terms. Where a row's terms bar commercial use, as IHME's do, and requirements.md does not settle that this analysis may use it, treat the row as an unset key under 3.0.3. Where its redistribution is no, keep the data out of the commit with a one-line .gitignore of `*` in its folder, and commit the entry and the query. Where it is unclear, commit it and say so in the entry, so that whoever shares the case can decide.
3.1 Seeds. List the two to five sources you recall or the design names for the input, and look each up by DOI: `.venv/bin/python research/bibliography.py lookup DOI`. Recall invents references, so cite none that has no record until you find it.
3.2 Two searches, each checked against the seeds as known items. The first is OpenAlex: `.venv/bin/python research/bibliography.py search --database openalex --limit 100 --log cases/<case>/search/search_log.jsonl "QUERY" > <your scratchpad>/search/<input>/search_1.jsonl`, the query naming the class of study in plain words. The limit of 100 is a cost cap for one input of a model, not a complete search: the log records the hit count OpenAlex reported, and a systematic review of the input (`notes/briefs/systematic_input_review.md` 3.5.1) takes every hit. The second is one web search for the same class, and one on the sites of the two or three agencies that publish on the input, such as a regulator's own analyses or a statistics office; write each query and the results you used to cases/<case>/search/<input>/web.md.
3.3 One step of citation chasing from each seed: `references DOI` (the works it cites) and `citing DOI` (the works citing it), each to a file of its own in your scratchpad. The raw results of 3.2 and 3.3 stay there and are not committed: the case keeps `search_log.jsonl`, each query as run with its count, and `web.md`, which is what the reporting standard for searches asks for, the strategies "copied and pasted exactly as run" and "the total number of records identified from each database" (rethlefsen_2021_prisma_s), not the records themselves. A seed cited more than 500 times (the lookup's cited_by_count) returns its first 500 citing works in OpenAlex's default order; say so. Then read the reference list in each stored seed's text.txt for works OpenAlex may not link, such as working papers and agencies' reports.
3.4 Merge the files, drop duplicates by DOI and the works you hold, and list the rest by title and year with the number of seeds each links to. A helper agent screens the titles against the input's definition; fetch and read the ones it keeps, and chase from any kept study that names another of the class.
3.4.1 Language. Search in English and in each language of the setting that `requirements.md` names; the search log names each language searched and the databases consulted for it, and says which local indexes (such as KCI or RISS for Korean) were not reachable. Never restrict by language without saying so. A source in another language is stored in its original, with its text; a quote is in that language, and any English reading is marked as your translation (CORE 1). A Korean, Japanese or Chinese source's key uses the romanisation printed on the work, else the language's official romanisation, the original-script name kept in the entry.
3.5 The paper says, for each input searched, the seeds, the queries as run, the counts and which seeds each search found, and for the rest that their sources were recalled, not searched for. It says what the search found, never that a class is complete.
3.6 Cost, for an input with three seeds: about 30 to 40 requests to OpenAlex, since each chase is a lookup and up to five pages; about $0.01 to $0.02 for the web searches; and about $0.02 to $0.06 for the helper to screen 300 to 1,000 titles, besides reading what it keeps. State any cost you report in dollars, to two figures, at the list prices of the shared price table (`shared/tools/token_prices.json`), never as a count of tokens (the owner, 2026-11-08: "I only care about price, not tokens"). If `research/bibliography.py` refuses because OpenAlex is closed in the ledger, do 3.1's seeds from the stored sources, the web searches and 3.3's reading of the stored reference lists, and say the OpenAlex search and chase were not run.
3.7 Two or more independent methods for each input that 3 searches. The owner's rule 9 asks for them: "We always look for two or more independent methods of estimating things", and "a structured agent process for brainstorming different approaches to estimating outcomes based on known data" (the owner's message of 2026-10-31). The options and their sources are research/independent_methods.md.
3.7.1 Your own list first. Once you have read the design, write down every way you can see to estimate each such input, one line each, before you read any helper's list. Ideas generated alone and then pooled beat ideas generated together (diehl_1987_productivity). The methods the design names are a starting list, not a closed one: a family a helper names that the design lacks is built, or dropped by 3.7.6 with its reason. Why: decisions/how-an-author-finds-independent-methods.md, attempt 1.
3.7.2 Two `method-lister` helpers, whose definition holds what each is asked, the three steps, the families of method and what each method states, dispatched in the background at the same time, each blind to the other and to your model, values, sources and list.
3.7.2.1 What each gets: the decision and the setting from design.md; for each input, its name, definition and unit; and one standpoint. One reasons as a scientist or practitioner of the inputs' field. The other reasons as an economist or statistician who measures such quantities. Never give a helper the design's section on methods, or any method the design names: the helpers are there to see past the design's framing. Do give each the menu of shared/library/access.toml (3.0), since a method that rests on data depends on knowing what data exist.
3.7.2.5 Write each return to cases/<case>/search/methods_<standpoint>.md, which your commits carry.
3.7.2.6 An input added after the first run goes to the same two helpers, resumed.
3.7.3 Choose. Merge the three lists. For each input, keep at least two methods that are independent.
3.7.3.1 Independent means they rest on different data and their main sources of error are unrelated, best when the errors would push in opposite directions (lawlor_2016_triangulation).
3.7.3.2 Two methods that share their data, their model or a key assumption are one method (knutti_2013_genealogy). Several studies of one kind are likewise one method (rule 9).
3.7.3.3 Each method kept is an input of its own. Look up and store the source of each figure it rests on, as 3.1 looks up a seed. Run the search of 3.2 to 3.4 only where the method's main effect rests on a class of studies, and then for that class alone. Where a method yields a quantity at another node of the model than the input, or one that already includes another input, adjust it to the input's definition before it is compared or combined, so that no pathway is counted twice or left out.
3.7.3.4 Where only one method can be built, say so, and say why each other method failed.
3.7.4 Compare before you combine. Run each method as its own input. Where their 90 per cent intervals do not overlap, the disagreement is a finding: look for the error that explains it, using the directions the lists give, and report what you found before combining.
3.7.5 Combine (rule 9), never by averaging the methods' draws or values.
3.7.5.1 By default, a mixture, each draw picking one method by its weight (cases/README.md, `mixture`). It fits rival accounts of which at most one is likely right, and its spread is wider than its parts' (gneiting_2013_combining), which is the honest reading of a disagreement.
3.7.5.2 The method's other option, "use where their distributions overlap", is not yet defined: it could mean the product of the methods' densities, or the draws of one method that the other does not rule out (research/independent_methods.md 3.2.4). Until the tool's method defines it, combine by the mixture alone; this is not a question to put to the case's user. Where the methods kept are unbiased measures of one quantity with independent errors, say so in the technical note, since the definition could then narrow the result.
3.7.5.3 Weights are equal unless there is evidence of which method is more accurate (armstrong_2001_combining). Equal weight goes to each independent method after 3.7.3.2's merging: variants within one method share its weight, so the number of variants kept never sets the mixture (knutti_2013_genealogy). Any other weight is your judgement, marked as yours. Where the weight could change the sign of the key result or the ranking of the options, it is set by a synthetic panel of experts, models answering the case's question for that weight with their answers pooled and labelled synthetic (`notes/case_pipeline.md` 6.2.1), and is never put to the user or the owner as a decision; every other weight keeps the rule above (the owner, 2026-11-13: "If it's important do a synthetic panel of experts to determine the weight"). A panel is shown the evidence and never a value computed for the weight it is asked, since a panel shown a number returns it. The panel runs by `tools/synthetic_panel.py` (its header): `write` makes a workflow in which each persona answers the weight alone at fixed probabilities with its reason, and `read` fits each answer and pools them by SHELF's equal-weight linear pool into one beta, labelled synthetic with its judges named.
3.7.5.4 Model averaging and stacking are used only where there are data against which each method can be scored (yao_2018_stacking).
3.7.6 Report it in the technical note, or at the middle tier in the technical appendix, and only there: the policy paper and the one-page summary show the one best guess from the weighted mixture, and the table of results by method stays in the appendix, since a reader of the paper needs one result (the owner, 2026-11-13: "This kind of thing goes in the technical appendix. The main paper shows our best guess for the weighted average of inputs"). For each input: the methods listed, with which helper or you listed each; the methods kept; one line for each method or family dropped, with its reason, a family the design lacked among them; each kept method's result with its 90 per cent interval; what explained any disagreement; and the combination, with why.
