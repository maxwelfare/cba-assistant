# House writing rules

A seat writes two kinds of document. A rule document says what the model (the simulation the project builds) or the working method should be: a specification, a design document, a roadmap, a team's index or an agent's memory. Agents new to the project work from it, so it stays current, plain and sourced. A document for a reader goes to the owner or to people outside the project: a paper, a report, a one-page summary, a sheet, a letter. Rules 1 to 12 govern rule documents; rules 1, 4, 6 and 10 govern documents for a reader as well, and rules 13 to 20 govern documents for a reader. A document editor writes under these rules and a reader checks a document against them. The owner directs the project; his rulings are cited as [OW-date-time], their identifier in his register, with the project named where the register is another project's. Each rule gives the norm, then the violation.

1. **A document says what is in place, as final text.** In a rule document, a decision that changes an entry (one rule, definition or table row) rewrites that entry in place; it is never stacked beside or after the text it replaces. (CORE 4.2: the commit that lands a ruling rewrites its home and every copy.) A document for a reader states what it is, since its reader never saw an earlier version; its revision history lives in git and the review records. A comment of the owner's on a document changes its content, and a question for him goes to him in the chat; a question for another audience (a committee, a client) goes where that audience's open questions are kept. A violation: an old value or definition kept with its replacement; a sentence narrating what changed, what used to be, what a draft or a reviewer said, or what supersedes what ("formerly", "now", "replaces", "was", "no longer", "closed in this revision"); a bracketed note to the reader, such as "[Draft note:"; a sentence that answers, defends or reassures about a change a comment of his asked for; or, in a document for a reader, an account of how it was made or checked (a list of the automated referees that reviewed it, a line that no person has checked it). A credit line or page notice he worded or ruled stays. The owner, 2026-09-29: "do proper edits instead of changelog accretion". On the same draft [the owner's message of 2026-11-21, ieq-modeling]: "get rid of the tags like "[Draft note:" the text should be for final review, raise questions to me here". On a passage of it [the owner's message of 2026-11-21, ieq-modeling]: "this smells like pointless text added because of a comment I made. This is a known bad habit of agents and please stop doing that". On a one-page summary [the owner's message of 2026-11-13, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "that wall of text under the title is full of academic caveats and process diary that have no place here", and "I will review these before they go out, kill the line about no human". That the comment and review-record clauses hold for every project's documents is the seat's reading of these words about single drafts.
2. **An entry says what the model is and includes.** A violation: an entry naming what the model lacks or is not ("no X", "never Y", "there is no layer called Z") where a statement of what it includes would carry the same rule. A negation stays only where it is itself the law, such as a constraint or a corner, and the text says why (the house's reading, not his words). The owner, 2026-09-29: "just say what things are and what the model does include".
3. **A specification or design document states what the model should do.** A violation: built or not-built marks, what the code does today, lists of the current code's state variables, step orders of the current code, milestone or version labels, schedules, and measurements of the current build presented as the rule. Build order belongs in the team's roadmap. A specification keeps the traceability table its field keeps, mapping each rule to the code that implements it, and that table is the one place its build status appears.
4. **Typos are always fixed, his quoted words included.** A seat fixes the typos in all text it writes, with no "[sic]" or brackets, and asks him where the word he meant is ambiguous rather than guess. The register of his words (the author's register) keeps his text exactly as typed, since it is the record. A sentence given as his words anywhere else keeps his wording, grammar and punctuation and carries the date; a rule resting on his words cites their identifier alone. The owner, 2026-11-20: "make it a standard rule that you always fix typos, and ask me if it's ambiguous". A violation: a typo left in any text, a paraphrase given as his, a quote under the wrong date, a guessed word where the intended one is ambiguous, or a rule going further than the words quoted for it. An agent's inference from his words is labelled as that agent's.
5. **Present and future tense only.** A specification describes the whole model in the present tense, ruled and built alike, as its field does (the owner, 2026-09-29: "follow the standard of the field, from context it is clear that this is not built"). A design document does the same, since rule 3 keeps build status out of it too (the house's reading, not his words). Every other rule document uses present tense for what someone has seen and future tense for what is ruled and not yet built. A violation: outside a specification or design document, a present-tense sentence describing a ruled plan as if it were built. A past-tense account of the project's work is the narration of rule 1.
6. **Plain language.** The terms of economics, agronomy, history and demography are welcome; project coinages, internal labels, code names and unexplained references (a pattern number, a row number, a version tag) are not. Plain does not mean long: the thing, the reason, stop. A violation: a label the sentence does not explain in plain words, or a conclusion that cannot be checked from what the document states.
7. **Numbered lists, never bullets.** Every list is numbered so each item can be cited. Tables stay tables.
8. **A quantity is named by what it is.** A violation: a single letter or an abbreviation standing for a quantity, where a name with its unit would say what it is.
9. **One home for each fact.** A violation: the same rule or number stated in two places of the document, or two places stating different rules for the same thing (a contradiction), or one word used in two senses, or two words for one thing. The fact stays in the section of its subject; elsewhere it is cited by number.
10. **Sources stay.** Every citation, research-file path and data-file path stays, with its tag ([A] anchored, [D] derived, [P] placeholder) where the document uses them. A sourced number stays, or moves to the data file that holds it and the document cites that file.
11. **A title starts with its distinguishing word.** A violation: a title opening with "The". The owner, 2026-10-01: "stop adding 'the' at the front of titles".
12. **A default never overturns a decision of the user's.** A default (the value a specification, schema or data file sets when nothing else decides) cites the decision or source it rests on, or is labelled the writer's choice. A violation: a default that contradicts a standing decision of the user's (a reader lists each one beside the decision it contradicts), or a default with neither a source nor that label. The reason: a default is where a decision is overturned without anyone deciding to, and a checker reading the text against itself does not see it.
13. **A number shown to a reader has two significant figures.** This holds for messages to the owner, documents and the result tables of tools: "about $93", "5,900 to 7,100 words", "$4.2 billion". A sound level in decibels keeps three, and so does any other log-scale quantity where the third figure matters. A number is rounded once, where a person sees it, and stored results keep every digit. The established rule is two effective digits (Ehrenberg 1977, `ehrenberg_1977_rudiments` in the cost-benefit project's `research/refs.toml`, recalled and not stored). What stays exact is the seat's reading: a verbatim quote, a count or price cited as its source's value, a date or year, a label, a commit or file name, and a count the reader checks by counting (15 tests passed). A violation: more figures than two outside those cases, or a decibel value cut to two. The owner, 2026-11-13 [the owner's message of 2026-11-13, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "make the output limit everything to 2 sigfigs". On a table of exposure values [the owner's message of 2026-11-21, ieq-modeling]: "good general rule but decibels need the 3. I can't think of any similar log scale where it matters like that but if there is one it also needs the detail".
14. **A table's body holds numbers only.** Each body cell holds a number, an interval where the table shows intervals, or a dash for none. Words (a condition, a caveat, a source, a qualifier) go in the row label (the stub column), the column head, or a footnote keyed by a mark, which is the stub, box head and footnotes of a statistical table (Chicago Manual of Style, ch. 3; ISO/IEC Directives Part 2, the clause on tables; both cited from a seat's memory, chapter and clause unchecked). A generator that writes tables refuses a body cell that is not a number (the seat's addition). A violation: words in a body cell. The owner's words of 2026-11-21 on a table of the ieq-modeling seed draft, relayed in `inbox/2026-11-21_tables-hold-numbers-only.md` and not yet found in a register: "screwed up by random pedantic text thrown in there. The table should just be numbers, any text goes in the left column or a footnote. this should be a general rule".
15. **Spelling follows the reader.** Messages to the owner and every document for American readers use American spelling: program, analyze, favor, center, percent. The audience profile or the requirements say who the reader is. That a document for a British or Australian reader keeps that reader's spelling, and that existing files in British spelling are not rewritten for this alone, is the seat's reading. A violation: "programme" or "analyse" in the chat or in a document for American readers. The owner, 2026-11-13 [the owner's message of 2026-11-13, automated-cost-benefit-analysis], of the chat and a report: "you are using British spelling here and in the report eg 'programme', in something designed for American Republicans".
16. **Authors are plain names.** A document's authors are written as plain names in the order the owner gives, with no "Dr." and no degree after the name; each author's degree, role and organization go in a short "About the authors" note at the end, as think-tank reports print them (Chicago and APA style). What the note says of a co-author is the owner's to give, and a detail that person has not confirmed is marked unconfirmed. A violation: a title or degree in the byline, an organization standing alone as author, or an author's name or detail guessed from an earlier document. The owner, 2026-11-12 [the owner's message of 2026-11-12, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "also use this format as the default for everything, so just my name on the virus panel report unless think tank reports to governments use a different pattern". A seat's lookup found no different pattern for reports to governments.
17. **Where two conventions collide, the familiar one wins.** The work follows the convention most of its readers know (a client, the public, the owner) and fits the narrower one around it; fitting it around is the seat's addition. This holds for sheets, documents, figures and tools alike. A violation: the familiar convention suppressed for a narrower one, such as hyperlinks left black so that blue means only an input cell, as the financial-modelling convention has it. The owner, 2026-11-21 [the owner's message of 2026-11-21, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "no everyone expects hyperlinks to be blue. Use normal hyperlink blue for them and a different blue for inputs", then [the owner's message of 2026-11-21, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "make a rule that if two conventions collide you use the one most people will be familiar with".
18. **Every hyperlink resolves before the document goes out.** A link checker run before a document leaves the project shows each link alive; a link refused by a bot wall (status 403 or 429) passes with a warning, and a dead link stops the document. A DOI or an institutional repository address is preferred to a department page, since department pages move. A violation: a document delivered with a link no check has opened. The rule is the seat's statement of the owner's rule of 2026-07-16 as the WelfareWeights memory `verify-links-before-shipping.md` records it ("never ship anything with a hyperlink without verifying it"), for which no register entry is found; the warning, the stop and the preference are that memory's.
19. **A delivered file is edited or replaced by the evidence of others' use.** A file is delivered once someone outside the seats has its link. Whether its new version edits it or replaces it turns on what replacing would destroy: links others hold, and their work in it (comments, notes, edits, tabs of their own, links into its cells). The model is version control's rule that history others have built on is not rewritten (Chacon and Straub, *Pro Git*, 3.6) and that a published address keeps working (Berners-Lee 1998, "Cool URIs don't change"). The steps below are the seat's reading of his words.
    1. The evidence is read first: the file's revisions, comments and sharing, and whether its link was sent. A file whose link was sent counts as seen even with no other trace.
    2. A file no one else has touched is replaced freely, and every link the seats wrote to it is updated.
    3. A file others hold the link to but have not worked in keeps its address, and the new version goes into it. Where that costs far more than a new file, the new file is made, and the old one stays, titled "SUPERSEDED", with a first line linking the new one; it is never trashed. A Google Doc delivered through the Drive connector cannot take new content in place (`update_file` replaces no content; the cost-benefit project, 2026-11-20), so the google-workspace skill's Docs route is tried first, and where none writes the body, the new version is a new file under this item's SUPERSEDED route (the seat's).
    4. A file others have worked in keeps their work, by a route that carries their comments and edits into the new version. Where no route keeps it, the owner is asked before the file is replaced, with what would be lost and whose it is. The old file is never trashed.
    5. The route follows the job's size (CORE 5.2.2): a few cells or words are edited in the file by hand, and a regenerated version goes through its generator and is written in one pass.
    6. An order of the owner's overrides steps 2 to 4, and the old file is still titled and points to the new one.
    7. After a replacement, every link the seats wrote to the old file is changed, and the owner is told who held it, from the commenters and editors the file names, since telling them is his to do.

    A violation: a delivered file replaced without reading the evidence, others' work lost, an old file trashed while others hold its link, or a rebuild done as a run of hand edits. The owner, 2026-11-20 [the owner's message of 2026-11-20, automated-cost-benefit-analysis]: "From now on it should be editing the existing sheet instead of making a new file each time. People will be working on it and linking to it." His words of 2026-11-21, relayed in `inbox/2026-11-21-acba-edit-or-replace-a-delivered-file.md` and not yet found in a register: "you can do better at creating sensible conditions for when to edit vs replace something that's been delivered. Base it on evidence of people looking at it. If nobody has opened it, then it can be replaced, But if people left comments in you need to preserve that or ask about replacing. In this case I ordered a new file even though comments were in the old one, because they went in too fast. But please actually think about good rules instead of blindly overfitting to my specific example of one of the many things a good rule should account for."
20. **A fault in a generated output is fixed in its generator.** A review of a generated sheet, PDF, figure or table reports each fault with the part of the generator that made it (script, template, data file) and edits no output; the fix lands in the generator and the output is regenerated by its normal route, since a hand fix leaves the generator wrong and the fault returns at the next run. A content change the owner asks for in a delivered file (19.5) is also made in the generator's input, so the next run keeps it (the seat's reading, joining his two words of 2026-11-21). A violation: a reviewer or seat editing a generated output by hand, or a hand-kept copy of a generated table. His words of 2026-11-21, on a layout reviewer that fixed a doubled minus by hand on a live generated sheet, relayed in `inbox/2026-11-21-acba-fix-the-generator-never-its-output.md` and not yet found in a register: "this should not happen. the process when finding a flaw in anything generated should always be to fix the generator, never hand edit one of its outputs".

## How a reader checks a document

1. Read the document in full, every line, against every numbered rule above that governs its kind.
2. Report each violation as one numbered finding: the line, the exact words, the rule's number, and the replacement text (or "delete"). A document with no violation gets "no findings".
