# Words that signal bad modeling

A reviewer of a brief, a plan or a rule document reads this list as an economist reads a referee checklist: each entry names a habit of thought that has produced bad models here, the words that usually carry it, the question to ask, and the form the field usually puts in its place. The words are a prompt to read carefully, never a match to fire on. A reviewer looks for the concept in any wording ("kicks in once", "after the body falls below", "can only"), and a word used in its legitimate sense passes when the document gives that sense its source: a physical bound, a law or custom of the world that is itself a step (an age of majority, a tax owed above a given income). A finding names the passage, the question, and the form that would replace it; it never refuses a document by the word alone. (The owner, 2026-10-10, asking for this list: "have a reader or critic question any brief that includes them. We had a list for the bentham oath but that was dumb text matching and we can do better as using them for a careful review not a simple message" [the owner's message of 2026-10-10].)

1. **A threshold.** Words: threshold, cutoff, trigger point, kicks in at, once X passes, below X then. Question: does the effect really begin at that point, or does the risk rise smoothly well before it? Usual form: a smooth dose-response or hazard curve (logistic or proportional hazard) fitted to sourced outcomes; the number named is at most a calibration target on that curve. His words: "If death hazard doubles at 0.7 then an increased chance of bad things will start well before then" [the owner's message of 2026-10-10].

2. **A number committed before calibration.** Words: a bare constant, "set to", "use 0.7", "about 30 percent" with no source. Question: is this a structural parameter with a source, or an observed outcome that should be a target the model is fitted to? Usual form: an observed outcome found among the inputs (a measured rate, a share, a level) is replaced by the mechanism that produces it, with a labelled best-guess coefficient derived once and never tuned to outputs, and the observed value becomes a calibration target (CORE 3.2).

3. **A cap on a behavioural quantity.** Words: cap, ceiling, maximum, at most, limit (of hours, of food, of spending, of effort). Question: is the bound physics or law with a source, or a stand-in for a declining marginal return?

4. **A choice's outcome made a state.** Words: off work, bedridden, stuck, cannot, unable, impossible, forbidden, must rest. Question: is this physically impossible, or is it what a person usually chooses at the prices the body or the world sets?

5. **Always or never in a rule about behaviour.** Words: always, never, every household, no one, all members. Question: is this a corner the objective produces at these prices, or a rule written in?

6. **A binary switch for a matter of degree.** Words: binary, on or off, yes or no, flag, mode, toggle, enabled. Question: is the choice truly indivisible, and if so is it convexified? Usual form: a continuous intensity, or for an indivisible choice a lottery over the discrete options (Rogerson's convexification).

7. **One mechanism for a social fact.** Words: the rule, the custom, everyone, universal, everywhere. Question: would a historian of two societies expect this to differ?

8. **"The" function or "the" worth.** Words: the value of, the utility of, the cost of, the preference for, stated once for everyone. Question: does this vary by culture, lineage or person?

9. **An averaged or representative member.** Words: average member, typical person, representative household, per capita stand-in. Question: does the mechanism depend on who in the household it is? Usual form (the seat's): each person carried as a person, with no averaged member [the owner's message of 2026-10-05: "'averaging people' is so dumb! It should never happen at all!"]. The same holds across a population: a harm per case taken from an all-age burden is applied to a subgroup (children, workers) only through that subgroup's own row, since an all-age burden can be mostly deaths of the old (ieq-modeling, 2026-11-19: a per-infection harm about 96 per cent deaths, mostly at 65 and over, against 290 child deaths of 45,000).

10. **A priority order standing in for prices.** Words: first, then, priority, before anything else, in order of need, lexicographic. Question: would the household ever trade a little of the first for a lot of the second? Usual form: marginal rates of substitution in one objective, so every use is valued by its marginal return per unit of resource (the seat's usual form; his words are "evaluate all choices by some kind of ROI based metric and assume zero interaction effects", the owner's message of 2026-10-05).

11. **A penalty, bonus or multiplier with no mechanism.** Words: penalty, bonus, multiplier, factor, weight, fudge, adjustment. Question: what real cost or benefit does this stand for, and where in the objective or the technology does it come from? Usual form: the cost or return derived from the mechanism, its parameters sourced.

13. **Certainty where there is risk.** Words: guaranteed, certain, will happen, deterministic. Question: what is the variance, and would a household facing it act differently from one facing the mean?

14. **A default or fallback for a missing value.** Words: default, fallback, if missing, when absent, otherwise use. Question: is the default itself data with a source, or does it hide a missing input?

15. **A lookup table between computed values.** Words: table of, interpolate, precomputed, cached curve. Question: is the table sourced data, or a stand-in for a law the model computes? Usual form: the law computed where it is used; a table only for sourced data (the seat's usual form; his words are "I am very skeptical that lookup tables shoudl ever be used", the owner's message of 2026-10-05, and "actual sourced data where we draw an input is tables", the owner's message of 2026-10-05).

16. **A search over options in place of a margin.** Words: try each, grid over, pick the best of, search. Question: is each option valued by its marginal return per unit of resource?

17. **A clamp in code.** Words: clamp, floor, `max(0, …)`, `min(1, …)`, bound to. Question: does the bound bind in normal runs, and if so what wrong law does it hide?

18. **"Realistic" or "reasonable" as evidence.** Words: realistic, reasonable, plausible, about right, feels right. Question: against which sourced band?

19. **A score its subject can lower by measuring.** Words: inspection, test, measured value, verified by. Question: can whoever controls the measurement's conditions lower the score, and is that path tested with a prepared arm as well as an honest one (Campbell 1979; Goodhart 1975)?

20. **A calculation entered as an input.** Words: an input that is a projection, a ratio, a value in a future year or a middle of analogs; "to reduce inputs"; an input budget. Question: could a reader look this number up as stated? Usual form: each input is a quantity a reader can look up (today's value, a growth rate, a measured effect, a judged chance), with its distribution, and a number worked out from inputs is a calculation shown where it is used; a count of inputs yields to this. His words (automated-cost-benefit-analysis, the owner's message of 2026-11-16): "no calculation should ever be entered as a primitive or input. That is a completely fake and wrong way to reduce complexity, and if we need to go over 100 inputs to do it right then go above."

21. **A fixed year for an uncertain time.** Words: "in 2060", "valued at", "as of", anchor year, start year. Question: is the time the result depends on known, or is it itself uncertain? Usual form: the start year, or the year a value is taken at, is computed from inputs with their distributions (CORE 3.1); constants serve only definitions and unit conversions (the cost-benefit seat's reading). His words, on a sheet that valued every year at 2060 (automated-cost-benefit-analysis, the owner's message of 2026-11-16): "it feels wrong to use 2060 as an anchor when the actual benefit is most likely in the 5 years between us and someone else" and "There should be no constants anywhere for anything uncertain!"

22. **A closed-form mean beside a simulation.** Words: expected value, exact mean, analytic, closed form, formula for the mean. Question: does every mean, share or interval reported come from the draws? Usual form: it does; a parameter needed to draw (an event's chance) is itself drawn, and a closed form appears only in a check that the mean of the draws lies within a few Monte Carlo standard errors of it (CORE 3.3; the usual form is the seat's). His words (automated-cost-benefit-analysis, the owner's message of 2026-11-17): "Any complicated Monte Carlo will make that a mistake especially when tails are involved. The mean of random draws is the only source of truth and anything else should go."

23. **A choice stated without its effect.** Words: we use, assume, by convention, set the rate at, chosen. Question: how do the outputs the reader acts on move under the alternative? Usual form: the choice is stated where it is made, with the alternative's effect on those outputs in one line, a side-by-side column or a row of a summary table (a one-way sensitivity analysis); a question to the owner about such a choice gives each option's effect. His words (ieq-modeling, the owner's message of 2026-11-19): "Basically every choice like this that might matter should be documented with a brief showing of how it changes important things."

24. **A distinction that barely moves the result.** Words: a separate schedule, column, period, occupant type, option or special case. Question: does dropping it change the result materially? Usual form: it stays only where it does, judged across the whole range of inputs the model is used over, not only at typical values (the seat's reading). His words, of a standard scored in points (ieq-modeling, the owner's message of 2026-11-21): "In general, anything that adds measurement or verbal complexity but only has a minor change in points should go."

25. **A second copy of an input.** Words: copied from, same value as in, a case's own data file. Question: does another case of the project, or a likely later one, use this input, source or model step? Usual form: it lives once at the project's root, every case reads it from there, and two copies found are reconciled into the root file in the same pass, since copies drift and a correction to one never reaches the other. His words (ieq-modeling, the owner's message of 2026-11-19): "make sure you are not making duplicate models ... that's the whole point of having this project, specific cases built on a shared foundation and data and assumptions".

26. **Cases or regimes for one judged quantity.** Words: soon case and late case, scenario, route, regime, bimodal, a chance of X then a pick. Question: does the world have separate attractors, with cited evidence, or did the author think in cases about one quantity? Usual form: one continuous distribution fitted to the judged quantiles (CORE 3.1). A split into regimes is built only with its evidence cited beside it, after it is put to him as a question on its own, never as a line in a longer report (the seat's rule, from the cost-benefit project's memory `one-quantity-one-distribution`: a per-draw pick between a soon and a late case reached a sheet as one bullet of a long report he never ruled on, and the late case carried about four fifths of the headline). His words (cost-benefit, the owner's message of 2026-11-17): "make sure all future predictions are single distributions unless we have really good proof that we are dealing with seperate attractors or regimes".
