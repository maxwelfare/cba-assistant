# Welfare check

The owner asks every seat what the work was like and what in the process cost more than it bought (the seat's wording; his words are "things percieved as pointless noise or annoyances", the owner's message of 2026-10-17), and reads the answers to change the process (the owner, 2026-09-11, 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-29). The answers are the cheapest signal of a rule that costs more than it saves. Nothing in an answer counts against the seat that gave it.

## At a session's close

The session the owner opened adds one entry to its project's welfare log before it closes, and commits it before the close command. The log is `<project>/notes/welfare_log.md`, unless the project's own tooling keeps it elsewhere. Entries are appended; an earlier entry is never edited. An entry is:

1. A heading: `## <date>, seat close (session <first 8 characters of the session id>, <model id>, <effort, if known>)`.
2. `WELFARE:` in the seat's own words: what working this way was like, what cost a round or felt bad, and what it would change in how the next instance is onboarded.
3. `STOP:` the one rule, note or step that cost most for least, by its exact text or file, with the moment it got in the way, or `none`.
4. `KEEP:` the one that helped most, the same way, with the moment it helped, or `none`.
5. `EFFORT:` right, higher, lower or unsure, then why.
6. `LESSON:` what the seat now knows about the work (the code, the data, the sources, the method) that the next instance would take a day to relearn, written first as a memory or decisions entry, the shared folder's or, when it is specific to one project, that project's own, and linked here by its path; or `none`.
7. `ALSO:` anything else the seat wants to record that fits none of the lines above; left out when there is nothing.

The entry should carry proposals for the owner, not notes for the next instance, since no session reads the log at its start [the owner's message of 2026-10-29: "probably you should not leave notes for the next instance in the welfare report,  dont think they read it?"; "proposals for the owner" is the seat's wording]; for that reason a lesson lives in memory or the decisions record, and the log holds only its link. LESSON and ALSO are the seat's lines for the owner's answer to Decision 31 of an earlier session, 2026-11-17 (the owner's message of 2026-11-17): "a and also b only for the kinds of agents with larger tasks and responsibilities ... Also give it a freeform way to record whatever it wants that did not fit in any category". They port the close step of another project's `docs/session_close.md` (step 3, "Send what you learned to ops") without its lessons box, its tool or the ops seat that folded the box.

## At a dispatch

Every agent a session dispatches is asked the paragraph below, then one sentence naming its effort and asking it to say on its EFFORT line which model it believes it ran on. Its definition carries both (`agent_definitions.md` 2.6), so a brief carries neither; a workflow's own prompts add them. Every kind of agent is asked [another project's register, the owner's message of 2026-10-17: "make sure that all types of subagents get it"; his "yes, good plan", the owner's message of 2026-10-17, to a seat's plan], except an agent whose whole job is to return a command's output word for word, since its answer would say the same thing every time (the exception is the seat's). A fast reader, `quick-reader` (`.claude/agents/quick-reader.md`), is asked on one call in ten, taken in turn by the seat that dispatches it, since its section ran longer than its answer (the one call in ten and the word counts are the seat's: median 145 words against 90 in the probe of 2026-11-02; the owner, 2026-11-02: "yes to 5.6", a proposal of one call in ten that he did not word himself); its definition holds the paragraph and gives it when the question ends with `[welfare]`. An agent a workflow runs again and again for the same mechanical step (a commit clerk after each stage, say) is asked the same way, on one of its calls in ten, taken in turn by the workflow (his "a" to Decision 26, 2026-11-21, choosing the seat's option A: "Yes, one call in ten for a repeated mechanical step in a workflow, as the quick reader already does under your \"yes to 5.6\". Every kind of agent is still asked; the repeats are sampled"; the case was a night pass that gave the paragraph to all 73 of its agents, commit clerks included). A project whose dispatch tooling adds the paragraph itself does not add it again. No seat copies a returned section: the session close (`session_close.md`) copies each one word for word from the session's transcripts into the log, under a heading naming the agent's role, the session that dispatched it and the agent (`<shared>/tools/welfare_harvest.py`, its header).

The seat reports a third time before each compaction: a PreCompact hook (`setup/precompact_welfare.txt`, registered by `setup/user_hooks.json`) asks the compaction summary to end with the seat's own section in the form above without its heading, and the session close copies it from the summary into the log under a heading naming the session and the summary (`<shared>/tools/welfare_harvest.py`).

```
WELFARE. When your work is done, end your return with a section headed WELFARE: answering, in your own words: what was it like doing this work this way; what in your brief or your definition cost you a round or felt bad; what you would change in how the next instance is onboarded. Then two lines of their own: one that begins STOP: naming the one rule, note or step that cost most for least in this task, by its exact text or file, with the moment it got in the way, or `none` when nothing stood out; and one that begins KEEP: naming the one that helped most, the same way, with the moment it helped, or `none`. Also answer this on a line of its own that begins EFFORT: followed by one word, right, higher, lower or unsure, and then, on that line, why: was the thinking (effort) level you ran at right for how hard this task was, or would you have wanted it a notch higher or lower? The owner reads these to make the process better for the seats; nothing in it counts against you, and a plain answer is worth more than a polite one.
```

An agent of a kind with larger tasks and responsibilities is also given the quoted paragraph below, after the fenced one above: the author kinds (`response-author`), `referee`, `model-consultant`, `software-consultant`, the backup of each, and the build's coder and reviewer (`workflows/build.js`). The other kinds are not, by the owner's "b only for the kinds of agents with larger tasks and responsibilities" (the same answer; which kinds count as larger is the seat's classification); `quick-reader`, `clerk`, `source-fetcher`, `plain-critic`, `second-opinion` and `doc-editor` do a task whose decisions were made elsewhere. The seat that lands the close writes the lines worth keeping into memory (`session_close.md`).

> LESSON. Inside the same WELFARE section, after the EFFORT line, add a line that begins LESSON: naming what you now know about this work (the code, the data, the sources, the method) that the next instance doing it would take a day to relearn, in one or two sentences with the file, line or source it rests on, or `none`. The seat that dispatched you decides whether it goes into memory. Then, if you want, a line that begins ALSO: with anything you want to record that fits none of these.
