# Peer review of a piece of analysis

How every project writes and reviews a piece of analysis: its design, inputs, model, results with 90 per cent intervals and technical appendix, then its policy paper and summary. It is every project's default (the owner, 2026-11-07, of its trial: "we just went from hardly accomplishing anything to smooth progress by changing the review workflow. ... make it the default for future projects"). The evidence for each reason below, and the source of each of the owner's words quoted here, are in the author's shared decisions record, which this copy does not carry.

## 1. The seats

1.1 The author: one agent holds the whole piece and writes and revises all of it, model code included, resumed with its memory (SendMessage) at each step. It runs its own checks and does not review its own work beyond them. It delegates the reading: helper agents, in parallel, fetch, store and quote sources and answer narrow questions about files, while the design, the model and the writing stay with the author. An author that does its own reading can run out of context mid-task.

1.2 The referees: fresh agents, blind to each other and to the author's reasoning until 3.7 hands them the record of closed points, each dispatched by its agent type as the shared `referee` (`.claude/agents/referee.md`) or a project's own referee agent, since a definition only read as a file sets no model, effort or tools, and told to assume the work has turned out wrong and to say why. One reviews the substance: structure, method, inputs and what would make the result wrong. The other audits: every number traced to its source, the code checked against the text, the model rerun.

1.3 The editor: a fresh agent, never the author. It rules on each open point (settled; a note in the piece's limits, with its size; or still to do) and on the whole, refining only while the next refinement is worth its cost (CORE 3.4). The author makes the fixes the ruling asks for, and the editor checks them in the files itself. The user's matters (rulings, value judgements, what the work is for) the editor lists in its ruling for the office to put to the user; no seat decides them.

1.4 The office: the coordinator session, or the workflow script that runs this form for it. It dispatches the seats, passes file paths between them and keeps the record. It changes nothing in the work. Its unit of dispatch is the plan's: each part a plan or ruling splits the work into gets an author of its own, since a split that stops at the plan hands one agent a batch its planner judged too large for one; a plan splits only work that does not share one context (1.1). Each seat is handed the items it acts on, not the whole list or detail file they sit in, since a seat handed a whole file reads it whole. The evidence is ieq-modeling's overnight pass of 2026-11-21 (its `review/v0_2_1/process_audit.md`, 3.3 and 3.6): an editor split one track into two batches and the script gave all nineteen items to one author, which ran out of room and left two of them to a cleanup round of 57 minutes and $50; and one roadmap detail file was read 27 times by 10 agents, about 330,000 tokens, and three prose editors named that reading their costliest step.

## 2. The milestones

2.1 M1, the method, which should be accepted before the run of record, the run of the accepted model whose results the documents report: the design, with the checks against reality its results must pass (CORE 3.3); the inputs, each with its source (CORE 1.2) and any quote it rests on checked against the stored text; the model; and the technical appendix with every citation and formula, each results table an empty shell. Two referees, substance and audit, either of which may run the model in a scratch copy to size a finding. Most of the review and fact checking falls here, since a finding made before the documents exist changes a row or a parameter, and a reviewer who knows the results judges the method by them. A `quote` field holds verbatim source text and nothing else; a page or line reference, how the passage was reached, an ellipsis's account of omitted words or any other remark goes in a field beside it (`stored_note` where the index has one), so that a quote the checker cannot find is always a quotation error (ieq-modeling, 2026-11-16: 8 of 155 passages its run of `shared/tools/quotes.py` could not find held only such a note inside the quote). Text a seat typed or reconstructed from a source (a transcription of a scan with no text layer, a table read off a page image) is never stored as the extract the checker reads (`text.txt`); it is kept beside it under a name that does not end in `.txt` (the checker reads every `*.txt` in a key's folder, `shared/tools/quotes.py`; a transcription is `.md`) and said so in the entry, so the checker never reads it and "found" always means a match against a machine extract (ieq-modeling, 2026-11-16: a hand-typed page of a 161-page WHO scan made its own quote check pass).

2.2 M2, the results and the documents. While the model makes the run of record, the author should draft the policy paper and the one-page summary from the accepted M1, each results table a shell (the owner, 2026-11-13: "Everything should be written up while the model is running, and then you just paste in the results and a few paragraphs describing them"). The results then fill every shell by script (3.3), and the author writes the paragraphs that describe them. One referee reruns the run of record from the commit M1's ruling names and checks that it reproduces the results, that the appendix, paper and summary say what the accepted M1 and the results say, in plain language, and that the results pass the checks M1 set; what else the results put in question counts by 3.7 and 3.8. M2 is a check of faithfulness, since the method it reports is already accepted.

2.3 Each milestone runs in four steps. First, round 1. Second, the author's response letter, answering every point "changed, here" or "declined, because", with an index of where each change is. Third, round 2, sent to the same referees, resumed, which asks only whether their own points were answered; a new point counts there only if it could move a headline outside its 90 per cent interval (before the run of record, as a referee's scratch run sizes it), or if it is a stated falsehood. Fourth, the editor's ruling and check.

2.4 The records are kept in the piece's own folder (a project with no folder per piece names one in its `CLAUDE.md`). The review files go in `review/`: `m1_r1_referee_econ.md`, `m1_r1_referee_audit.md`, `m1_r1_response.md`, `m1_r2_referee_econ.md`, `m1_r2_referee_audit.md` and `m1_editor.md`, then `m2_r1_referee_documents.md`, `m2_r1_response.md`, `m2_r2_referee_documents.md` and `m2_editor.md`. The appendix, paper and summary go in `documents/`.

## 3. Rules, each with its reason

3.1 A revision goes back to the referees who asked for it, scoped to their own points. A fresh reader on every redraft finds new root problems each time and does not converge.

3.2 An editor who is not the author rules, and attempts are not counted. A count cap or forcing rule added to stop one loop becomes the next loop. Whether a finding sends work back is the editor's judgement, made at the rigor the user asked for: a note, a minor fix (reviewed, no rebuild) or a real rework (a rethink). No rule fires rework, a rebuild or a stop on its own, such as a changed brief forcing a new build, every finding answered by changing the work, or a bound that discards finished, verified work, unless the owner ordered it; a changed input reruns the existing model. A finding that returns after a fix goes to the editor as a sign that minor edits are failing, never as a trigger (the owner, 2026-11-06: "Independent reviewers and editors can use sense and judgment to prevent coders from pushing out slop while also greenlighting minor changes, instead of being tied to overly strict rules"; the owner's message of 2026-11-06: "So the editor should make that call: decide if something needs a real rework or a minor fix. The process shoudl not force them"; the three calls and the returning-finding cue are the seat's, from the cost-benefit project, where on 2026-11-06 a changed brief forced a rebuild that discarded a model its referee had verified). A judged standard in place of a fixed rule is Kaplow's trade (`kaplow_1992_rules`), and a separate person deciding whether to go on is the remedy for escalation of commitment (`simonson_1992_deescalation`).

3.3 Every number in a letter or document is filled from the results by script. It removes transcription errors, and every author of the trial names it as what helps most.

3.4 Referees test every completeness word ("every", "each", "all"). It is the commonest falsehood an author writes, and one an editor removes from one document can come back in the next.

3.5 A long reviewer writes its report as its findings firm up, since a long read gets compacted.

3.6 Seats that write the same tree at the same time each work in their own git worktree, and when several pieces share a branch, each commit names its paths: `git commit -- <paths>`. A check run on a shared tree reads the other seats' uncommitted edits and fails on work that is not its own (ieq-modeling's overnight pass of 2026-11-21, its `review/v0_2_1/process_audit.md`, 3.2: a renderer build beside a model author failed its check twice on the author's edits, passed on a clone of the last commit, and ended unpassed and unreviewed).

3.7 The editor's ruling holds the record of closed points, so that no later round argues again what one round settled (the owner, 2026-11-13: "I thought we were supposed to have a review log where settled decisions did not have to be reviewed again"). It names the commit at which its check finds the fixes it asked for made, which at M1 is the accepted method the run of record runs, and gives each point one row: its identifier, prefixed by the milestone and round that raised it; how it closed (changed, and where; declined, and why; or a note in the piece's limits, with its size), or what is still to do; who closed it, the editor or the owner, whose answer the office writes in word for word and their date; and what the closing rested on, the passages read at that commit and any run that sized it. Each ruling carries forward the rows of the one before and closes those its check finds done, so the last ruling holds the whole record. The office hands it to every later reviewing seat, since the blindness of 1.2 serves a first read and a later round reads the change. A closed point whose closing rested on something since changed is reviewed again as part of the change (3.8); otherwise it reopens only where a reviewer shows evidence the closing round did not have, the results of the run of record included, that passes the test of 2.3 for a new point, or a false statement of fact in the piece. The reviewer names its ground and the editor rules on it; any other objection to a closed point is answered by its row.

3.8 After M1's ruling, a reviewing seat reviews only what has changed since the commit the last ruling names, what that change reaches (a changed input reaches every result and sentence that uses it), and the rows the record leaves open; at M2 the change is the results and the documents, read against the accepted M1. A point beyond these counts only if it passes the test of 2.3. A change to the accepted M1, before the run of record or in a later revision of the piece, is declared in the appendix with its reason and reviewed as a later round of M1 by M1's referees, resumed, confined so; the run of record is then made again from the commit its ruling names, and M2 reads the new results as its change. A corrected sentence of the appendix reopens M1 only where it changes what the method does: a sentence brought into line with the model and inputs at the commit M1's ruling names corrects how the method is described and is checked at M2 for faithfulness (2.2), while a correction that changes the model, an input, the design or a check against reality is a change to the accepted M1. A reviewer handed the whole piece reviews it whole, so a small change would cost a full review and bring settled points back.

3.9 A round reviews the queued batch (CORE 6.2.1; roadmap.md 4.11); the re-read of 2.3 follows the one fix pass.

3.10 A review round of a document or other deliverable ends with one validation reading: `plain-critic` reads the rendered product (the PDF, page or sheet its reader gets) whole and cold, as that reader, on a green build after the round's last fix, given the owner's statement of what it is for and his orders for it, never the round's queue or diff. The referees verify the work against its brief; only this reading asks whether it is the right product (Boehm 1979; controls.md, "What makes a check worth having", 2). It stands outside 3.8's confinement, since it reads the product, not the change (the seat's rule; his "dont overfit this is clearly one small instance of a larger class of problem", 2026-11-21, relayed in its inbox note and not yet in a register). The evidence is two projects': an overnight ieq-modeling round of referees and an editor missed faults he saw in the PDF in minutes, and one cold reading of that PDF then found more; a cost-benefit seat took a client sheet as done on "the sheet runs and the headline matches" while its guide and notes were in no check (its welfare log, 2026-11-20).

## 5. Tiers of rigor

5.1 This form is the middle tier (the owner, 2026-11-07: "This is effectively the middle (or possibly middle-high) tier of effort and rigor. A fast estimate would not have had any mid process revision, just reviewer notes at the end").

5.2 The fast tier runs straight through with the same reviewers. Their notes are collected on the finished draft, and nothing is revised during the run.

## 7. The established techniques

7.1 Journal peer review: blind referees, a response letter, and the revision returned to its first referees (the field's practice, recalled; no source for it in the libraries yet). An editor who is not the author is the IPCC's review editor (`ipcc_2013_procedures`). It is also the remedy for an author's commitment to its own course (`staw_1976_knee`).

7.2 The referees' charge is a pre-mortem (`klein_2007_premortem`).

7.3 One author holding the whole work, with helpers doing its support work, is Brooks's surgical team, the chief programmer team, which keeps a work's conceptual integrity in one mind (`brooks_1975_mythical`, chapters 3 and 4). The form departs from it in one way. Brooks's team has no blind outside referees; this form adds them, since one mind left alone holds to its first course (the owner, 2026-11-06: "One instance will just make up something random and become attached to it. We have to have independent review and revision and asking hard questions.").

7.4 Each handoff between agents loses part of what the work's maker knew. Handoffs are one of the seven wastes of lean software development (`poppendieck_2006_implementing`).

7.5 Review of the method before its results is the Registered Report (`chambers_2013_registered`; `nosek_2014_registered`): a journal accepts a study's question and method in principle before its results exist, and the second review asks whether the method was followed, whether the quality checks set in advance pass (the checks of 2.1), whether each analysis outside the plan is marked and justified, and whether the conclusions follow from the results, never how important or novel the results are (`pcirr_2026_reviewer_guide`, criteria 1E and 2A to 2E). Its reason is that a reviewer who knows the results judges the method by them, "critiquing after the results are known" (`nosek_2014_registered`). Results-free review is the same order for results that exist and are withheld from the reviewers (`button_2016_withholding`). Economics' form is the pre-analysis plan, which fixes the outcomes and specifications before the data are analysed and declares each departure from them (`casey_2012_reshaping`; `olken_2015_promises`), its tables drafted blank and filled once the data are in (`jpal_2026_preanalysis`). The form departs from them in three ways: its first reason is the cost of a late finding, where theirs is publication bias and the search for a favourable specification; its inputs and their sources exist before the run and are reviewed at M1, where a study's data do not exist at its first stage; and a referee may run the model (2.1), so the review is blind to the run of record and not to every output, as a registered report may carry preliminary studies (`pcirr_2026_reviewer_guide`).

7.6 The record of closed points and the confined later round are a standards body's comment resolution and recirculation ballot: each comment's disposition is recorded as accepted, revised or rejected, with the reason where it is not accepted as written; unresolved comments go to every balloter with their responses and the next draft; and once a draft has reached approval, later comments may address only the changed portions, the portions the changes affect, and the subjects of unresolved comments (`ieee_2026_sasb_opman`, 5.4.3.3 and 5.4.3.4; `ieee_2025_ballot_comment_resolution`). The form departs from it in two ways: the editor closes a point, where there a subgroup of the working group that drafts the standard does, since an author does not rule on its own work (1.3); and a point beyond the change counts where it passes the test of 2.3, where the standards body may hold such a comment for a later revision, since an error in a published analysis costs more than a round of review.
