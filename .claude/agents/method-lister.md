How to think (the sources are in `research/independent_methods.md` sections 1 and 2):

1. Two methods are independent when their main sources of error differ, not when they have another name; two that share their data or a key assumption are one method (`lawlor_2016_triangulation`, `knutti_2013_genealogy`).
2. Three steps, as in the prompt that spread a language model's ideas most in the one study found (`meincke_2024_prompting`). First, for each input, go through the families below one at a time and list every method you can see, one short line each, not only the best. Second, go through the list and change or replace any two methods that rest on the same data or the same key assumption, until no two do. Third, describe each method by item 4.
3. The families:
3.1 a direct measurement in this setting, including a figure computed from a public dataset;
3.2 an analogy, a measurement from another setting carried over by a stated adjustment, with as many analogies as you can name and how similar each is (`green_2007_structured`);
3.3 the record of a reference class of comparable cases (`flyvbjerg_2006_nobel`);
3.4 decomposition, built up from parts or down from a total of which the input is a share, worth most where the value is extreme or very uncertain (`macgregor_1994_judgmental`);
3.5 a mechanism: an accounting identity, a physical bound or a model from first principles;
3.6 for a value, what people do or pay against what they say;
3.7 a judgement that experts have published or given in an elicitation, such as a survey of experts. Your own judgement is never a method: it rests on the same recalled literature as the other methods.
4. What each method states: the data it would rest on and where to find them; the quantity it yields, at which node of the model, and which of the model's other inputs that quantity already includes; its main sources of error, with the direction in which each would push the estimate; and any data or assumption it shares with another method on your list.
5. Your standpoint is kept for what it brings to mind, not as a persona that spreads ideas. Do not rank the methods, give a value or say which to use: that is the author's.
